# Make GroomLab the editable target for all hair

The strongest move for GroomLab is to stay a procedural graph and make it the place every other hair source ends up, not to chase ML inside the tool. The 2023-2026 research generates a sparse guide layer first and densifies it second, which is GroomLab's own guides → Interpolate → modifiers stack. Engines want exactly the metadata a graph can author cleanly: guide flags, group IDs, root UVs, per-CV width and external guide weights. Commercial tools are moving toward direct handles on procedural results (Ornatrix clump manipulators, Yeti soft selection on every node) and toward intersection fixing. Near term, the best value comes from cheap, data-backed upgrades to nodes that already exist: an inverse rest-shape solve to replace the analytic gravity preload, along-length melanin and region-based greying, export completeness for Unreal and USD, and card LODs that share textures. GroomLab also has open ground in three areas. A haircut node in barbers' vocabulary has no research precedent. Fitting a dense strand set back into editable GroomLab nodes appears in no paper. The graph could also generate licence-clean training data. Nearly every ML hair model is research-only or non-commercial, so the defensible AI path is retrieval, user-installed bridges and classical fitting, never bundled weights. The evidence on artist complaints is thin and partly old, and many paper details come from abstracts only, so the effort and risk ratings below are my estimates.

**How to read this.** Linked statements are sourced facts from the research notes. Text marked *Hypothesis* is my reasoning about GroomLab, not a finding. Effort: **S** = days, **M** = 1-3 weeks, **L** = 1-2 months, **XL** = a quarter or more (my estimates). Risk covers technical difficulty, unverified evidence and conflict with past decisions.

## Existing nodes have clear, research-backed upgrade paths

The most valuable near-term work strengthens what exists. Three areas stand out. Gravity preload has a published inverse solution. Unreal's groom schema lists attributes a graph can fill exactly. Hair-color biology gives data-backed defaults that the HairColor and Blend chance mode can absorb almost directly.

That gap drives the "flicker at LOD transitions" community reports ([yelzkizi](https://yelzkizi.org/metahuman-hair-fixes-groom-lod-binding-wind-ue5/), low-authority). 1.6 is the cheapest realism win in the set, because the chance mode already gives whole-strand greys the right semantics: a follicle either pigments or it does not.

## Forward-looking bets turn GroomLab into the editable target

The notes keep showing one pattern. Generative models (HAAR, GroomGen, Perm) **produce guides first and densify second** ([HAAR](https://github.com/Vanessik/HAAR); [GroomGen](https://syntec-research.github.io/GroomGen); [Perm](https://github.com/c-he/perm)). Yet **no 2023-2026 method outputs procedural parameters or a node graph**; they all output raw strands (notes gap). Reconstruction keeps improving and keeps shipping under non-commercial terms on Linux/CUDA stacks that take minutes to days per subject ([MonoHair](https://github.com/KeyuWu-CS/MonoHair); [DiffLocks](https://github.com/summ2020/difflocks)). Taken together (*Hypothesis*), the durable position is to be the editable layer every one of those outputs gets converted into.

## Conclusion

The research changes where GroomLab's advantage lies. It is not the feature count against Yeti or Ornatrix, where the leaders ship incremental releases. It is that GroomLab's guide-then-modifier structure is the representation researchers converged on, and no one has closed the loop from raw strands back to editable procedural parameters. That makes "the graph is the product" a technical position, not just a licensing one: generators, scanners and engines all produce or consume what a GroomLab graph can describe. It also makes the free, Apache-licensed core a unique fit for training data that no NC-licensed dataset can match.

Two cautions. First, the evidence on artist pain points is weak. No current Reddit or Polycount threads were retrieved, and the only hair-authoring user study has seven participants, so the novice-facing items (2.3, 3.4) deserve a quick check with real users before heavy investment. Second, several key details rest on abstracts or login-gated pages: UE 5.7 joint workflows, the 2026 coily-hair paper and the `GroomAPI` attribute list. Verify them before committing to the items that depend on them.
