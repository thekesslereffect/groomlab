Guides are the curves you shape by hand; the render strands are interpolated between them. The
basic workflow:

1. Open **GroomLab > Groom Tools**, pick **Place** and click or drag on the scalp.
2. Shape the guides with **Comb**, then refine them with the other Shape brushes.
3. Select some guides and choose **Edit Guides > Children** to generate child guides inside their
   tubes.
4. Continue brushing; the strands update with every stroke.

## The hierarchy

Primary guides are the top level. **Child guides** are generated inside each parent's **tube**
(the volume around a guide in which its children are placed), grandchildren inside the children's
tubes, to any depth. A child stores its shape relative to its parent, so editing a parent moves
and reshapes all its descendants.

- **Generate children:** select guides, set the count in the box beside **Edit Guides** (in the
  picked group's box under **Guide groups**) and choose **Edit Guides > Children**. Only the
  selected parents get children (with no selection, the group picked in the list). Select children
  and repeat for grandchildren.
- **Place children by hand:** click a child group in the **Guide groups** list and use Place or
  Draw; each new child is parented to the nearest guide of the parent group.
- **Restructure:** **Reparent...** (move the selected children under another parent) and
  **Promote / Demote...** (move a group one level up, or under a sibling group) in a group's right-click menu
  change the hierarchy while keeping the guides' shapes.
- **Segments:** the field in the picked group's box under Guide groups shows the point count of
  the selected guides, else of the picked group's (of every guide with All groups picked). Dragging it resamples them
  live; releasing applies it as one undo step. Frozen guides keep theirs. Resampling a whole group
  also sets the segments its new guides get from then on: drawn, placed, from curves, baked, and
  children generated into it (the group's tooltip shows it). Selected guides resampled get a count
  of their own: the group's count changing later leaves them as they are, until you resample them
  to the group's count again, or right-click the group > **Reset Segments** (every guide of the
  group back to its count, frozen ones kept). Give guides more segments before combing tight curls.
- **Which guides drive the strands:** by default Interpolate uses each group's deepest level.
  Where a group has children, the strands are interpolated from the children, and the primaries
  shape the strands through them; a group without children drives the strands itself ([Interpolate](/docs/node-graph#interpolate-and-guide-influence)).

## Tube guides

Every guide has a **tube**: the volume around it that its children are generated in and that
Interpolate's **Fill Tubes** fills with strands. Shaping tubes gives anime and cartoon hair, Pixar
style locks and spikes: each lock is shaped as a whole and its strands stay inside it to the tip,
with no mesh to model.

A tube is one **shape** carried along the guide, changed along the way by its **sections**:

- **Shape:** `round`, `ribbon` (a rounded rectangle), `teardrop` (round against the scalp, pointed
  away from it) or `blade` (pointed at both edges), or a **custom shape** from the groom's shape library
  (below). One per tube. Picking a shape for a guide lays its tube flat on the head: at twist 0 its
  width runs along the scalp, across the way the guide lies, and its height off it.
- **Width:** half the section's width, in scene units (a round tube's radius).
- **Aspect:** the section's height over its width. 1 draws the shape as designed; toward 0 it
  flattens into a ribbon.
- **Twist:** the angle the section is turned to at that point, in radians (not accumulated: 0 at
  the root and 3.14 at the tip turns it half a turn along the guide).

Width, aspect and twist are each a list of values spaced evenly from root to tip, blended between
(one value applies along the whole guide). The tube turns with the guide: its sections follow the
guide's frames, so bending a guide carries its tube and everything inside it.

**Sections follow the head.** A shaped tube's sections also turn to lie as the scalp does, all
along the guide: where the guide runs over the head (within a couple of section widths of the
scalp), twist 0 keeps the section's width along the scalp and its height off it, however the guide
curves round the head, so a ribbon combed over the head stays flat on it instead of drifting on
its side. Farther from the scalp the sections carry on as they left it (a lock hanging off the head
keeps its way), blending smoothly in between; twist turns the section from there. A plain round
tube is left as it is (its children and strands as before).

**Custom shapes (the shape library).** Each groom has a library of custom tube shapes, saved with
its guides (in the scene and in `.groom` files). A custom shape is an outline drawn by control
points in the section's square, -1 to 1 across and up with the guide through the middle: a smooth
curve through them (**Smooth**, the default) or straight edges with sharp corners. Guides take a
library shape by name just as they take a built-in one, and everything follows its outline exactly
as with a built-in: children are made inside it, Fill Tubes fills it, clumps stay in it, the wires
draw it. Editing a shape changes every guide using it at once (one undo step). An outline must not
cross itself and must surround its center (where the guide runs): edits that break either are
refused with a message saying what to fix. Saved points are fitted into the square, so a shape
drawn small comes back filling it. A guide whose library shape is missing (a hand-edited file)
loads round, with a warning.

**Children follow the tube.** Children are generated inside the shape's outline, not a disc, and
keep their place in its sections: flatten a tube and its children flatten with it, twist it and
they twist, taper it and they gather. A child's own tube is its parent's, narrower (**Tube scale**),
lying the same way. Children of a shaped tube whose guide leaves the head at a slant root on its
footprint (the root section laid flat on the scalp, as its strands do), spread over the section's
area rather than squeezed onto a line across it; guides within 30 degrees of standing straight up
keep their children's roots as before, and plain round tubes keep theirs.

**Setting up.** Fill Tubes on the Interpolate node is all it takes (the **Tube locks (anime)**
preset, under Hair in the Presets menu, has it on, with soft clumps that follow the tubes): place
guides, then shape their tubes with the Tube brush. A Clump after it follows the tubes by itself.

**Fill Tubes.** Turn on Interpolate's `fillTubes` and every strand rooted inside a guide's tube's
**footprint** (the root section laid flat on the scalp round the guide's root, so it covers the
section's whole area however steeply or shallowly the guide leaves the head) takes its place in the
tube's cross-section and keeps it from root to tip: the hair fills the tube exactly, flattening,
twisting and tapering with it, and is as long as the guide. Within a few widths of the root each
strand rises from its footprint into the tube. Strands rooted in no tube are interpolated from the guides
as usual, so tubes can cover the whole scalp or only some locks. The tubes of **every guide level**
fill, whatever level the Guides node hands Interpolate: where tubes of several levels hold a root
(a child's tube inside its parent's), the deepest one takes the strand, so the child's tube fills
its own footprint and the parent's tube the rest of its. A Guides node's `exclude` leaves its
groups' tubes out too. Where tubes of one level overlap, a strand takes the tube it lies most
centrally in. Strands in a tube carry `tubeId` (which tube), `tubeU` and `tubeV` (their place in
its section, -1 to 1).

**Tube Blend** (Interpolate's `tubeBlend`, 25% by default) is the tubes' soft edge, as a share of
each tube's half-width at its root. A strand rooted just outside a tube, within that distance of
its outline (measured on the footprint, equally thick all round however flat the tube),
leans from its usual shape toward riding the tube: all the way at the outline, none at the band's
edge, smoothly between. So there is no seam between the hair filling a tube and the hair around
it, nor round a child's tube inside its parent's. Strands past the band are exactly as without
Fill Tubes. 0 gives a hard edge.

**Clumps in tube space.** A Clump after it (with `followTubes`, on by default: nothing to set)
picks each tube's clumps among the tube's strands and clumps a strand only toward its own tube's
centers, so clumps stay inside the tube and flatten, twist and taper with it.

**New guides take the chosen shape.** A guide you place or draw (symmetry's mirror guide too) gets
the shape chosen in the **Tube** block of the Place and Draw cards (a built-in or a library shape;
round until you pick one), and, with **Match nearby** on, its width, flatness and twist blended from the two nearest guides'
(the nearer counting more), so a new lock starts as thick and as flat as the locks round it. With no
guides around (or Match nearby off), it takes the block's **Width**, **Flatness** and **Twist**.
In Python: `brush_tool("place", tube={"shape": "leaf", "width": 0.1})` (and `brush_stroke(...,
tube=...)`); `placement_tube()` reads it back.

**Scatter fills tube footprints.** A Scatter with **guideRadius** keeps roots only near the guides;
round a guide with a shaped tube that means its footprint (the root section laid on the scalp, plus
**tubeMargin**, a share of the half-width as Tube Blend measures; 0 by default: exactly the footprint) instead of a disc round
its root, so a wide blade's or a ribbon's footprint fills evenly at the scatter's density, upright
or lying low. Guides without a shaped tube keep the disc of guideRadius.

**Shaping tubes in Maya.** Pick the **Tube** brush (**Groom Tools > Tools > Tube**). While it is
on, the tubes of every guide it can shape (shown and not frozen) draw as wires: the outline of each
section at every guide point, where the brush paints, and lines along the tube through the shape's
corners (a blade's points and crests, a ribbon's corners, a teardrop's point), so the shape reads.
The selected guides' tubes draw brighter.

- **Width, Flatness, Twist** (in Maya at the top of the **Brush** block). Drag over guides to paint their tubes near the brush, each
  section by the brush's falloff (and the **Root to tip** curve) at its guide point. **Width**
  grows the tubes (a negative strength shrinks them), **Flatness** flattens them toward a ribbon
  (negative: rounds them back), **Twist** turns them about the guide (negative: the other way). The first
  stroke on a tube gives it one section per guide point, so you shape it point by point.
  Symmetry, frozen and soloed guides work as with every brush; each stroke is one
  undo step.
- **Shaded** (off by default) draws the tubes as shaded surfaces, open at the tip, easier to judge
  a tube's root, mid and tip profiles; **Wireframe** (on by default) draws them as see-through
  wires, over the surfaces when both are on. In Maya they sit under **Visibility**
  (`groomLabBrushCtx -tubeShaded true -tubeWireframe false`); in Blender and the app at the top of
  the **Profile** block.
- **Always visible** (Maya, the end of **Visibility**, off by default) keeps the tubes drawn as
  set there with every other tool too (Comb, Select, ...), not only the Tube tool
  (`groomLabBrushCtx -tubesAlwaysVisible true`).
- Selecting tubes with Ctrl+Shift solos them: the other tubes stay drawn, dimmed (their wires and
  shaded surfaces dark), and so do their guides, so the selected ones, orange, stand out.
- The Tube tool's card has no **Applies to** block: its **Mirror** is at the bottom of **Brush**.
- **Colors** (Maya, on by default) colors each tube in its root tube's color (see Root tubes
  below), or, a tube of no root tube, in a color of its own. With **Shaded**
  on the color goes on the shaded surfaces and the wires stay gray; with it off, on the wires.
  The selected tubes' wires stay orange. (`groomLabBrushCtx -tubeColors false` turns it off.)
  While the Tube tool's tubes are drawn, the guides of root tubes are drawn in their root's color
  too, not their group's (a split tube's and every child guide's: the color of the level 1 tube it
  comes from).
- **Ctrl** does the opposite: it shrinks, rounds or untwists the tubes.
- **Shift+drag smooths** what the brush paints: the channel picked (width, flatness or twist) of
  the sections under the brush, each evened out toward its neighbours along the guide. The
  guides' shape stays as it is. (With the other guide brushes Shift+drag smooths the guides.)
- **The tool cards' tube blocks.** Each guide keeps its own tube. The Shape and Select cards have
  **Profile** and **Selected guides**, which edit **only their targets**: the selected guides
  (select them with **Select**), or with none selected the soloed ones (left unfrozen while the rest
  are frozen). They follow every change of the selection or solo.
  - **Profile**: three pickers, **Root**, **Mid** and **Tip**, each with its own **Apply**. Pick a
    shape (the built-ins, then the library's; **Mixed** while the targets' differ there) and every
    target takes it; **Apply** gives the row's shape to all of them again. The tube's outline changes
    smoothly from the root's shape to the mid's (halfway along) and on to the tip's, the outlines
    matched along their edges so each profile is exactly its own shape at its place. The **Root**,
    **Mid** and **Tip** buttons pick which profile the shape editor shows. Under each row, **Length**
    (in percent) sets how much of the tube is exactly that profile's shape (the root's from the root, the mid's
    around the middle, the tip's up to the tip); between the held stretches the shape changes
    smoothly, and where two of them meet it changes at once. **Mid** set to **Between** changes
    straight from the root's shape to the tip's; **Tip** set to **As root** comes back to the root's
    shape. The strands filling the tube, its children and its clumps all follow the
    changing outline. Under the pickers, the shape editor shows the profile picked last. With no
    guides selected or soloed, the block lists the profiles the groom's guides use (and how many
    guides use each): pick one to edit it in the shape editor, and every guide using it follows,
    as the root, mid or tip profile. The app and Blender have the same rows (Blender's shape editor shows the root's
    profile; the library list picks another shape to edit).
  - **Selected guides**: the targets' **Width** (the tubes scaled so the first one's widest section
    is that wide; their taper and painted detail kept), **Flatness** and **Twist** (the same from
    root to tip). **Smooth** in the block's header evens out the targets' tubes along their guides:
    the **Current channel** (the one the brush paints) or **All channels** (its arrow's menu).
  - **Tube** (the Place and Draw cards): the shape new guides get, with a small preview, and its
    **Width**, **Flatness** and **Twist** (used where no guides are near to blend from). Profiles
    are edited with the Tube brush.

  Each change is one undo step. **Fill Tubes** and **Tube Blend** are the Interpolate node's
  settings (`fillTubes`, `tubeBlend`), in the Graph Editor.
- **The shape editor** (in the Profile block): a square showing the chosen shape's outline, the guide
  at the cross, its height (off the scalp) up. A built-in shows read-only: **Duplicate** (in the block's header) copies
  it into the library as a new shape (the targets take it). A library shape's points drag (the
  groom shows the change live while you drag; letting go saves it, one undo step, every guide using
  it following); **click the outline** between two points to add one; **right-click** a point to
  remove it (three stay at least). **Mirror** (on by default) keeps the left and right sides alike:
  a point and its mirror image move, come and go together, points on the middle line stay on it.
  **Smooth** switches between a smooth curve and straight edges. The block's header has the
  library's glyphs: **New** (a circle to start from), **Rename** (type the name in place, Enter),
  **Duplicate** and **Delete** (click twice; its guides become round). None of these opens a dialog.

**Seeing tubes.** Turn on **Display Tubes** in the groom's Attribute Editor (`displayTubes`; in
the app, **Tubes** in the Display panel) to draw every guide's tube with its shape and sections.

**From Python** (Maya), one undo step per call:

```python
import groomlab
# A flat ribbon that turns a quarter turn and tapers, on two guides.
groomlab.set_tube(groom, guides=ids, shape="ribbon", width=[0.4, 0.1], aspect=0.2, twist=[0.0, 1.57])
groomlab.set_tube(groom, group="primary", samples=8)  # 8 sections along each tube, to shape finely
groomlab.get_tube(groom, ids[0])  # {"shape": "ribbon", "width": [...], "aspect": [...], "twist": [...]}
groomlab.scale_tube(groom, 1.5, guides=ids)  # every width times 1.5, the taper kept
groomlab.smooth_tube(groom, guides=ids, channel="flatness", passes=3)  # or "width", "twist", "all" (None)
groomlab.set_fill_tubes(groom, on=True, blend=0.25)  # Fill Tubes and Tube Blend on every Interpolate
groomlab.fill_tubes(groom)  # {"fillTubes": True, "tubeBlend": 0.25}, or None without an Interpolate
groomlab.brush_stroke(groom, "shape", points, radius=0.3, tube_channel="flatness")  # a scripted stroke
# The shape library: custom outlines, (u, v) control points in the -1..1 square, in order round it.
groomlab.add_tube_shape(groom, "leaf", [(0, 1), (-0.6, 0.2), (0, -1), (0.6, 0.2)], smooth=True)
groomlab.set_tube(groom, guides=ids, shape="leaf")  # guides take it by name
# Root, mid and tip profiles: the outline blends root to mid to tip ("" unsets the mid or tip).
groomlab.set_tube(groom, guides=ids, shape="round", mid_shape="leaf", tip_shape="blade")
groomlab.set_tube(groom, guides=ids, root_hold=0.2, mid_hold=0.1, tip_hold=0.3, mid_at=0.6)  # where each holds
groomlab.set_tube_shape_points(groom, "leaf", points)  # every guide using it follows (smooth= too)
groomlab.tube_shapes(groom)  # the built-ins and the library: name, builtin, points, smooth, users
groomlab.rename_tube_shape(groom, "leaf", "petal")
groomlab.remove_tube_shape(groom, "petal")  # its guides become round; returns how many
groomlab.preview_tube_shape(groom, "leaf", points)  # shown live, nothing stored (end_guide_preview)
groomlab.tube_targets(groom)  # what Profile and Selected guides edit: the selected guides, else the soloed ones
```

`set_tube` takes `guides`, `group` or `level` (default: the selected guides) and any of `width`,
`aspect`, `twist`, `shape`, `mid_shape`, `tip_shape` and `samples`; what you leave out stays. The command underneath is
`groomLabGuides -setTube <json>` (its keys: `shape`, `midShape`, `tipShape`, `width`, `aspect`, `twist`, `samples`,
`scaleWidth`, and `smooth` (`width`, `aspect`, `twist` or `all`) with `passes`) and `-tube <id>`;
the library is `-tubeShapes` (a query, JSON), `-editTubeShape <json>` (`{"op": "add" | "set" |
"rename" | "remove", "name", "points", "smooth", "to"}`, one undo step) and `-previewTubeShape
<json>` (live, no undo step), and `-tubeTargets` gives the panel's targets. The brush takes
`-placeTube <json>` (Place and Draw) and `tube_channel` (`width`, `flatness`, `twist`;
`-tubeChannel` on `groomLabBrushCtx` and `groomLabBrushStroke`). Fill Tubes and tube-space clumps
run on the CPU; the GPU preview hands those nodes to it. The shape editor is in Maya and the app;
Blender has the same blocks and lists the library, without the outline editor yet.

### Tubes drawn root first (Maya)

The Tube tool's **Tube roots** section draws a tube the way Tonic does: its root first.

- **Draw root**: click the root's outline on the scalp, one CV at a time (drag to move the CV
  just placed; **Undo CV** takes one back). Near another root tube a CV snaps onto its CVs or edges
  (**Snap to neighbours**), so the tubes meet with no gap. Clicking the first CV again closes the
  outline.
- With **Draw guide before extrude** (on by default) the root's middle then shows as a CV: drag
  from it out to draw the tube's guide, as the Draw tool draws one. The stroke is smoothed over a
  quarter of the root's size, so the hand's shakes go while bends, curls and spirals about as wide
  as the tube stay. The guide's points are spaced evenly, as many as its length and turns need
  (about one per root width, and no span turning more than 15°), times **Point density**: a short
  stroke gets few. A stroke leaving the root nearly flat along the scalp starts at most 50° off the
  scalp's normal and bends into it. Where the stroke dips into the head (it is drawn on the view's
  plane), the guide is popped out there, a third of the root's size over the scalp (from nothing at
  the root to all of it two root sizes out), the lift spread smoothly along it, so it rises gently
  over the scalp while its bends, curls and course elsewhere stay as drawn.
  On release the tube is made and the next root can be drawn. Off, the tube goes straight off the
  scalp, **Length** long. Undoing the tube just made (Ctrl+Z) brings its outline back, ready to
  draw its path again.
- The tube's root section is exactly the drawn outline (straight edges between the CVs; a library
  shape of its own, `rootTube_<n>`, removed when the last guide taking it is deleted), narrowing
  to **Tip width** of it at the tip. The tube is swept from its root along the path with no
  twist: its sections don't roll to lie along the scalp as other shaped tubes' do. Its root
  footprint (where Scatter and Fill Tubes put the tube's hair) is the outline drawn, fixed on the
  scalp: laid along the scalp itself, not the guide, so combing or leaning the guide never turns
  or moves it. It is made 1.5% larger so sealed neighbours overlap a little rather than leave a
  crack; a strand in the overlap goes to the root it was drawn inside, one in a crack or just
  past an edge to the nearest root, and one its root's split tubes leave out to the nearest of
  them.
- Each root tube keeps its own hair with no region map: Fill Tubes gives every strand rooted in a
  root's area to that root's tubes, and Clump's **followTubes** keeps it in that root's clumps.
  (A region map you paint yourself still works as usual, for the strands outside every tube.)
- A root's CVs are kept on the scalp itself, apart from its guide: combing, brushing, retwisting
  or resizing the tube never moves them, nor the area its hair roots in. Only **Edit roots**
  changes them.
- Every root shows on the scalp in its own color, the same as its tubes and guides are drawn
  (**Draw root** shows the other roots' CVs too, dimmed, the points a new CV snaps onto, and edges
  the root being drawn green; entering it with a deeper level of the tube hierarchy picked shows and
  picks level 1, where a new root tube's guide belongs),
  half see-through, with its CVs as large dots drawn over its edges. The roots sit in the scene
  like the scalp: the hair and the guides in front of them hide them. A root being edited or
  selected has thicker green edges; its CVs stay the same.
- **Edit roots** shows the root tubes' outlines, filled, and their CVs (the selected guides'
  tubes, or every root tube with none selected), the guides hidden. Drag CVs on the scalp:
  - A CV shared with a neighbour moves with it.
  - With **Snap to neighbours** a CV snaps onto another root's CVs and edges, never onto its own
    root's.
  - **Ctrl+click an edge** to add a CV there, on every outline sharing that edge.
  - **Ctrl+click a CV** to take it out of the root edged green only; a neighbour sealed to it keeps
    its own. A root keeps at least 3 CVs.
  - **Undo CV** takes back the last CV move. Maya's Ctrl+Z undoes the scene, not a CV move; a root
    it changes shows as the scene now has it.

  The roots whose CVs you last touched are edged green. **Finish edit** moves each changed tube to
  its new root, refits its section (its taper and what brushes painted kept) and updates the
  regions, in one undo step. A root that can't take its new outline keeps its old one and the
  status line says why; the edit finishes either way. Leaving the tool any other way (another
  tool, **Draw root**, **Select root**) finishes the edit too, so no edit is lost.
- **Select root** shows every root's middle. Click one to select its root (edged green), Shift+click to
  add roots or take one out, and drag a selected one to move them all over the scalp as one; with
  **Snap to neighbours** their CVs sealed onto a root not selected stay put while the others
  slide. Their tubes and hierarchies move with them on release, in one undo step. **Duplicate**
  makes the selected roots follow the cursor over the scalp; a click sets copies down there, with
  copies of their tubes and the tube hierarchies under them. **Delete** removes the selected roots,
  their tubes and hierarchies.
- Clicking **Draw root** or **Select root** again leaves that mode; **Finish edit** applies the
  edits and leaves (Ctrl+Z takes them back in one step).

#### Subdividing a root

Draw one big root, then let GroomLab fill it with smaller ones. One row under **Duplicate** does
it: **Subdivide**, the paint brush, the **Multiplier** and **Apply**.

**Subdivide** cuts the selected roots (**Select root**) into Voronoi cells. Each cell becomes a
root tube of its own in the tube hierarchy's next level (subdivide a level 1 root and its cells are
level 2), following the root you cut: its guide and tube, still there at level 1, shape them all. Neighbouring cells share their CVs, so they seal with no gap, and
the cells along the edge keep the old root's CVs, so they stay sealed to the roots around it.
Each cell is a slice of the old tube: its root is its part of the old root, its guide starts in
its middle and runs inside the old tube, and its sections are the old tube's scaled to it and
turned as the old tube's, so the cells keep its shape, spread, taper, flatness and twist and fill
it side by side, a slight gap apart, never passing through each other. If the old root had a tube
hierarchy under it, each cell is split as deep, afresh. It is one undo step. The first
**Subdivide** makes the groom's one density map, `tubeSubdivisionDensity`, at 0.5; every cut reads
it.

The paint brush beside **Subdivide** opens the Paint tab on the density map (it is there once a root
is cut). White (1) gives more, smaller roots that fit tightly, for example at the bangs. Black (0)
gives fewer, larger ones. Ctrl paints toward black. The brush again, or leaving the Paint brush any
other way (Q in Maya, another tool), takes you back to the Tools tab and the Tube tool; nothing is
cut until you press **Apply**. The **Multiplier** makes the cuts denser overall with the painted
density: 1 as painted, 2 about twice the roots. **Apply** cuts the subdivided roots again all over,
at the multiplier and the map as they are. It keeps your work: edits to the cut roots' outer edges
(**Edit roots**) are kept, since Apply fills the outline the cells' outer edges make now, and the
new cells take the shape the cells near them were groomed to (combed, drawn) and their point
count, with their tube hierarchy made again under them. A cell can be cut again (**Subdivide** on
it): that is a destructive cut, which the map and **Apply** leave be, keeping its place. The groom
remembers each cut (saved with its guides), so you can come back and paint again later.

```python
from groomlab import root_tubes
guide = root_tubes.make_root_tube(groom, outline_points, path=None, length=2.0, taper=0.5)
root_tubes.edit_root_tube(groom, guide, moved_points)
root_tubes.update_regions(groom)
cells = root_tubes.subdivide(groom, [guide], root_tubes.DENSITY_MAP, multiplier=1.0)["cells"]  # Voronoi cells, each a root tube
name = root_tubes.ensure_density_map(groom, root_tubes.DENSITY_MAP)  # the one map, made at 0.5
root_tubes.apply_density(groom, [], name, force=True)     # every cut by it again (Apply)
```

### Tube hierarchy

The Tube tool's **Tube hierarchy** block subdivides tubes, level by level. Its row holds a button
a level on the left and **+** and **−** on the right (when the levels grow that far, **+** and **−**
move down to a row of their own). For a drawn root, **+** cuts its root in two as well (each half a root of its own, with its
part of the root's CVs, sealed to the other half: pick, edit or subdivide it at its level; stepping
through the levels shows that level's roots), and slices its root shape in two across its
longer side: each half a tube of its own filling its half of the parent tube, so the two never pass
through each other unless groomed so, and what you paint on a tube (Width, Flatness, Twist) carries
to the slices under it. A drawn root's tube always starts on its root drawn: the Tube brush shapes
it from there up, never the root itself. **+** splits each
selected tube (or, with none selected, every tube of the base group) into two tubes side by side
across its longer side, sharing its root shape, in the next level's group (`<base>_L2`,
`<base>_L3`, ... up to level 9); pressing it again splits those in two (2 to 4, and on).
The numbered buttons show one level at a time (the other levels' groups hidden) and pick its group,
so the Tube tool and the brushes edit that level's tubes. **Lock view**, under them (off at
first), keeps the tubes of the level shown when you switch it on in view while you pick other
levels to work on, whose guides show instead: step down to level 1 and comb its guides while level
5's tubes stay in view and follow. It only locks the levels'
view; the other visibility controls work alongside it. **Mixed view**, under it (on at first),
also shows, while you work on a level, the tubes (not the guides) of the levels above it where no
level below continues them: a level 1 tube that was never split shows while you work on level 2.
Select root and Edit roots show those levels' roots too. Every level's tubes follow their parent:
shaping a level 1 tube carries all the smaller tubes inside it, while each smaller tube keeps its
own edits. **−** undoes a split: with tubes selected, only theirs (a split tube loses its deepest split tubes; a split tube goes back into its parent with its siblings), with none the deepest level everywhere. **+** likewise splits only the selected tubes when some are. A slid guide stays its own tube's clump center wherever its root moves within its root. Every tube split from a root tube, and every
guide under it, belongs to that root, so the smaller tubes' hair stays inside the root they came
from. Fill Tubes fills a strand rooted in a
level 1 tube only with that tube's own smaller tubes, never a neighbour's (a split tube's corners
may reach past its level 1 tube's outline).

```python
root_tubes.add_tube_level(groom)                   # every leaf tube split in two
root_tubes.show_tube_level(groom, "rootTubes", 2)  # level 2 shown and returned
root_tubes.show_tube_level(groom, "rootTubes", 1, locked=6)  # level 1's guides, level 6's tubes
groomlab.generate_children(groom, "tubes", count=2, split=True)  # the split itself (-split true)
```

The Tube tool's card is long, so each of its sections (Brush, Paints, Profile, Selected guides,
Tube roots, Tube hierarchy, Applies to) folds away: click its chevron or its title. Which sections
are folded is remembered when you switch tools and the next time Maya starts.

## Guide groups

Guides belong to named **guide groups**. A group is either a top-level group of primary guides
(`primary` by default, `braids`...) or a child group whose guides are children of the guides in
its parent group. Groups are listed in **Groom Tools > Tools > Guide groups**, under the tool card
(always open): one row per group with its color dot, its name (indented under its parent), its
guide count and an eye. The section header shows how many there are, **Isolate** (the eye) and
**+** (a new group). Under the list, the picked group's box has **rename**, **select** and
**delete**, its **Segments**, and **Edit Guides** with the children count.

| Action | How |
| --- | --- |
| Create a group | **+** in the section header (**New Group**): a name and a parent (**None** for a top-level group, or a parent group for a child group). |
| Make it the active group | Click its row in the list. Brushes only edit the active group's guides, and Place and Draw add guides to it. In Maya, clicking the picked group again goes back to **All groups**, which brushes every guide. **Page Up** / **Page Down** step one level up or down. |
| Rename | Double-click the name, or **rename**. Guides node params that name the group are updated. |
| Show / hide | The row's eye. The guides' eye at the top of the Groom Tools hides or shows every group of the groom at once ([The current groom](#the-current-groom-and-what-shows)). |
| Color | The row's dot shows the color the group draws in: filled when it has a color of its own, a ring for its automatic one. Right-click the row > **Color...** to pick a color, **Default Color** to clear it. Every group without its own color, child groups too, gets a hue of its own, well apart from its parent's and its siblings'. To see a painted guide layer instead, set **Guide Color** (Display > Guides) to **Painted Layer**. The automatic colors start from a hue of the groom's own, from its name (the Maya transform, the Blender object, the app's file name), so two grooms side by side differ; the same name gives the same colors in Maya, Blender and the app. |
| Select its guides | **select**, or right-click > **Select Guides**. |
| Delete | **delete**: the group, its child groups and their guides. The dialog lists graph nodes that use the group. |
| Mirror | **Edit Guides > Mirror > Group...** copies a top-level group to the other side. |

**Isolate**, the eye in the Guide groups header, draws the active group and its child groups at full color and
dims every other group.

**Hidden groups** are not drawn, not affected by brushes and not picked by the Select tool. Hiding
does not change the groom: the guides still drive their strands. Hiding applies to the named group
only, not to its child groups.

In the graph, groups are referenced by name: a **Guides** node's `group` and `exclude` params
choose which groups feed Interpolate, and a **Group** node with `by` `guideGroup` makes a strand
mask from the strands nearest a group's guides ([Restricting a node](/docs/node-graph#restricting-a-node-to-some-strands)).
Groups, and the guides in them, are saved with the scene and in `.groom` files; hidden state and
group colors are stored on the Maya node (`hiddenGuideGroups`, `guideGroupColors`) and not in the
`.groom` file.

```python
groomlab.list_guide_groups(groom)                      # [{name, parent, depth, count}, ...]
groomlab.add_guide_group(groom, "bangs", parent=None)
groomlab.rename_guide_group(groom, "bangs", "fringe")
groomlab.show_guide_group(groom, "primary", visible=False)
groomlab.set_guide_group_color(groom, "fringe", (1.0, 0.5, 0.2))
groomlab.select_guides(groom, group="fringe")
groomlab.guide_ids(groom, "fringe")                    # the group's guide IDs
```

A guide's group is set when it is created (the active group for Place and Draw, the child group
for Edit Guides > Children). There is no command to move existing guides to another group.

## Freeze and solo

Freezing protects guides from brushes. The freeze state is a per-guide value from 0 to 1, the
**freeze mask**; brushes scale their effect on each guide by 1 minus its freeze value, so a
partial value gives a partial effect. Child guides inherit their parent's freeze value. Frozen
guides are drawn blue. Freezing does not affect the strands.

- **Freeze** (a brush in the Guides category) paints the freeze mask; **Ctrl** unfreezes.
  **Unfreeze All**, next to it, clears the mask on every guide.
- **Select** solos: the selected guides stay live, all others are frozen, and a click on nothing
  unfreezes all.
- **Solo new guides** (Place and Draw settings, off by default): each guide you place stays live
  and all others are frozen, so you can shape it immediately.
- With **Symmetry** on, the mirrored guides are soloed too. Each of these is one undo step.
- **Freeze exact guides:** the freeze mask is a guide map named `freeze`. Select guides, then in
  the **Paint** tab pick `freeze`, set **Value** to 1 and click **Fill**, which fills the selected
  guides only. From Python: `groomlab.freeze_guides(groom, guides)` (the selected guides by
  default; `value=0` unfreezes) and `groomlab.unfreeze_all(groom)`.

To freeze a whole group, select its guides (**select** in the group list) and fill `freeze`, or
in Python `groomlab.freeze_guides(groom, groomlab.guide_ids(groom, "fringe"))`.

### Detailing at higher density

A typical way to work from coarse to fine:

1. Groom the primary guides with the `primary` group active, until the overall shape is right.
2. Generate children (**Edit Guides > Children**), then make the child group active. Brushes now
   only affect the children, and the primaries stay as they are.
3. Hide the primary group if its curves get in the way, or turn on **Isolate** to dim it.
4. Solo areas while detailing: with the **Select** tool, drag over the guides to work on, and
   everything else is frozen. A click on empty space releases them.
5. Editing a primary later still carries its children, with their detail, along with it.

For more density than hand-placed children, interpolate dense guides procedurally with a
**ToGuides** node and edit them with a **Sculpt** node, whose edits survive upstream changes
([Other main nodes](/docs/node-graph#other-main-nodes)).

## Brushes

Select the groom and pick a brush. The radius is in screen pixels, strokes act in screen space,
and each drag is one undo step. The brushes are the same, in the same order, in Maya, the
[app](/docs/app) and [Blender](/docs/blender).

| Category | Brush | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Create | **Place** | Click for one guide, drag to place several (at least **Spacing** apart). New guides take the shape of the nearby guides (**Match nearby guides**). |
| | **Draw** | Press on the scalp and draw the guide's shape. |
| | **Part** | Draw a part line; interpolation does not blend guides across it. |
| | **Flow** | Draw direction arrows and whorls for short hair without guides (see below). |
| Shape | **Comb** | The main shaping brush: drags guides along the stroke with roots pinned. With **Stretch** on, combing also lengthens or shortens them. |
| | **Slide** | Moves roots along the scalp; the guides keep their shape. |
| | **Smooth** | Removes kinks (**Straighten**: relaxes guides toward a straight line instead). |
| | **Lift**, **Swing** | Raise guides off the scalp; turn them around their root. |
| | **Attract** | Pulls tips together (negative strength pushes them apart). |
| | **Tube** | Paints guides' [tubes](#tube-guides) near the brush: **Width**, **Flatness** or **Twist** (a negative strength: the opposite); its card's **Profile** and **Selected guides** set the selected guides' tube shape, width, flatness and twist. |
| Trim | **Cut**, **Length**, **Erase** | Cut guides where the brush touches; lengthen or shorten them uniformly; delete guides, part lines or flow arrows (not the scattered hair: see below). |
| | **Length Keys** | Scale guide lengths from keys you place on the scalp, blended smoothly between them (see [Length Keys](#length-keys)). |
| Paint (in the Paint tab) | **Paint** | Paints the map picked in the Paint tab, including the maps that drive settings such as density and width (see below, and [Painting maps](/docs/painting-maps)). |
| Guides | **Select**, **Freeze** | Select guides; paint the freeze mask. **Unfreeze All** is next to them. |

**Painting a setting** (density, width, length, clumping...). In the Paint tab, click **+ New Map**
and set **For** to the setting: **Density**, **Width**, **Length**, **Clump strength**, **Curl**,
**Noise amplitude**, **Flyaways**, or **Other setting...** for any other mappable parameter. GroomLab
creates a white map (so nothing changes yet), connects it to the setting, selects it and starts the
Paint brush on it, all in one undo step. If the setting already reads a map, that map is selected
instead. A stroke paints toward the **Value** (1 is the setting's full value); **Ctrl** paints
toward 0: thinner hair on the width map, no hair on the density map. For example, to thin the
sideburns, create the map for Density and Ctrl+paint over them.

**Erasing hair.** The **Erase** brush removes guides (with their children), part lines and flow
arrows, so it does nothing on a groom whose hair comes from the Scatter alone. To take hair away,
paint the density map holding **Ctrl** (Paint tab > **+ New Map** > For: **Density**); paint it
back without Ctrl.

### Length Keys

**Length Keys** (Trim) scales guide lengths from keys you place on the scalp: longer on the legs,
shorter at the paws, a fringe that runs from short to long. Each key holds a scale (x1.5: half as
long again). Between keys the scale blends smoothly over the whole scalp, measured along the skin,
so a key on one side of an ear does not reach through to the other. There is no reach to set:
each key's influence ends where its neighbours' begins, and away from all of them the scale eases
back to x1, so guides far from your keys stay as they are.

- **Click** on the scalp to place a key. A new key takes the scale already there, so placing one
  changes nothing. The first key is x1: a hold. Put x1 keys round an area to fence it in.
- **Drag** a key to move it over the scalp. **Ctrl+click** deletes it.
- **Right-drag** a key up or down to scale it (Maya: **middle-drag**, since Maya's right button
  opens its marking menus). Dragging up doubles it every 150 pixels.
- The guides preview at their new lengths until you **Apply** (one undo step; **Enter** in the
  app and Blender) or **Cancel** (**Esc**).

Each key is labelled with its scale and the length it gives the guide nearest it. The **Keys** box
shows the selected key's **Key scale** and **Key length** (type a length and the scale is set to
match), **Scale** or **Trim** (a guide scaled about its root, so curls keep their shape, or cut and
extended at the tip, so curls keep their size), **Delete Key** and **Clear**. Only the selected
guides change, else the soloed ones (the ones not frozen). With symmetry on, the mirrored keys act
too and show on the other side; topology symmetry puts each key at its partner spot on the scalp.

The keys are saved with the groom. Apply bakes their scales into the guides and sets the keys back
to x1, so you can come back, drag a key and apply again without scaling twice.

### Keys

- **Ctrl** does the brush's opposite: Length shortens, Lift lays guides down, Twist turns the
  other way, Attract pushes apart, Shape shrinks, Cut grows. Painting, it paints toward 0 (Ctrl+Paint on the density map removes hair,
  Ctrl+Freeze unfreezes). Comb, Slide, Smooth and Erase have no opposite.
- **Shift+drag** smooths with any brush: with Comb, Length or another guide brush it smooths the
  guides (as the **Smooth** brush, at the brush's radius and strength); painting maps or the
  freeze mask, it smooths the paint; with the **Tube** brush, the tube channel it paints.
- **Ctrl+Shift+drag** with a Shape or Trim brush (Comb, Slide, Smooth, Lift, Swing, Attract,
  Tube, Cut, Length, Erase) paints guides into the selection: the guides the brush passes over
  are added, and when you let go every other guide is frozen, so the next strokes work on those
  alone. Drag over no guide to clear the selection and unfreeze everything; a click on nothing
  changes nothing. Press the two keys in either order. The **Select** tool still has its lasso,
  box and click (see [Freeze and solo](#freeze-and-solo)).
- **Alt+Shift+X** turns symmetry on or off, after **GroomLab > Install GroomLab Hotkeys**.
- **B** + drag sets the radius, **M** + drag the strength, after **GroomLab > Install GroomLab
  Hotkeys** (or middle-drag and Shift+middle-drag).
- **Page Up** and **Page Down** step the active group up or down a level.
- **Alt+S** opens the [radial menu](/docs/app#the-radial-menu), after **GroomLab > Install GroomLab Hotkeys**.
- Tablet **pen pressure** scales the strength.

**GroomLab > Help > Keyboard Shortcuts** lists every key (the same table as the app and Blender) and
marks the ones that need the GroomLab hotkey set. **Install GroomLab Hotkeys** shows which keys it
changed and the hotkey set it switched from.

## The current groom and what shows

One groom is **current**: the Groom Tools, the Graph Editor and every brush work on it.

- **Pick it** in the list at the top of the Groom Tools or in the Graph Editor's groom list: both
  move together. **Selecting a groom** in the viewport or the Outliner makes it current too. A
  picked groom stays current while you select a scalp, curves or anything else.
- **Pin the Graph Editor** (the pin beside its groom list) to keep it on its groom while the Groom
  Tools move on to another one; its list then changes only the graph. Unpin it and it shows the
  current groom again. The pin lasts for the session.
- **The eyes** beside the list: the first shows or hides the **hair** (the strands, and whatever
  the groom grows on them: instances, scales, cards, strand meshes; the groom's **Display Hair**),
  the second the **guides** (every guide group at once, with their tubes, dots, selection, parts
  and flow arrows; the groom's **Display Guides**). Each leaves the other as it is: hide the hair
  to see the guides alone, the guides to judge the hair. Both are viewport switches: renders and
  exports always have the hair. **Shift+click** an eye, or right-click it for **Show All** /
  **Hide All**, to do it for every groom in the scene.
- **GroomLab > Display** has **Show All Hair**, **Hide All Hair**, **Show All Guides** and
  **Hide All Guides**. With the GroomLab hotkey set, **Alt+Shift+G** turns every groom's guides
  off, and on again. (To hide a whole groom, hide its node as any other: **Ctrl+H**.)

groomlab.set_current_groom(groom)        # what a pick in either list does
groomlab.active_groom()                  # the current groom

groomlab.set_all_guides_visible(True)    # every groom's (GroomLab > Display)

## Brush settings

The Groom Tools scroll as one panel. At the top are the groom header, the tabs and the tool grid: labeled tiles
of one size, as many to a row as the panel's width holds (at most four; on the Paint tab, one
full-width **Paint** button over the maps). Right under the grid is the tab's list:
**Guide groups** on the Tools tab, the maps on Paint, the layers on Correctives, the photos on
Photos. The picked tool's **card** sits under the list: only its settings (hover a tile for the
tool's whole hint and the gestures), in blocks, always in this order and each only when the tool
uses it:

1. **Brush**: how the stroke feels.
2. The tool's own block, named after what it sets: **Paint** (Mode, Value and **Fill**, the
   stamp), **New guides** and **Tube** (Place, Draw),
   **Visibility**, **Profile** and **Selected guides** (Shape; [tube guides](#tube-guides)),
   **Selection** (Select), **Erases** (Erase), **Keys** (Length Keys), **Flow** and **Parts**.
3. **Applies to**: where the stroke lands.

With no tool picked, the card says how to start.

| Setting | Block | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Radius**, **Strength** | Brush | Brush radius (pixels) and the amount applied per dab. **Strength (±)**: a negative strength reverses the brushes that work in both directions. |
| **Falloff** | Brush | How the effect fades toward the brush edge: 0 hard, 1 soft from the center out. |
| **Smooth** | Brush | Painting: how much Shift+drag smooths per stamp (past 1: several passes). |
| **Pressure** | Brush | A tablet's pen pressure scales the strength. |
| **Keep off scalp** | Brush | Guides never go below the scalp; points pushed under it slide along it. |
| **Stretch** | Brush (Comb) | Combing lengthens or shortens guides as you pull, instead of keeping their length, like ZBrush's Snake Hook: points are added as a guide grows and removed as it shrinks, so its segments keep their length. |
| **Falloff curve** | Brush | The falloff as a curve, center (left) to edge (right), shown under the chips while it is on. Right-click it for presets. |
| **Root to tip** | Brush | How much of the strength each part of a guide receives, root (left) to tip (right); for example, style the ends and leave the roots. |
| **Guides / Parts / Flow** | Applies to | What a shaping brush moves: guides, part lines, flow arrows. |
| **Mirror** | Applies to | Symmetry: mirrors every stroke across world **X**, or with **Topology** across the scalp's own left and right (for a head that is rotated, animated or not quite symmetric). **Alt+Shift+X** toggles it (in Maya, with the GroomLab hotkey set). |

A stroke never reaches what is on the far side of the head from the camera (guides, parts, flow
arrows or paint), and brushes work on every unfrozen guide of the picked group; to brush only some
guides, Ctrl+Shift+drag them into the selection (the rest freeze) or freeze the others. How the strands
draw while you sculpt is the groom's setting: Attribute Editor > **Sculpting** (the app:
Properties > **Sculpting**; Blender: **While sculpting** under the Groom Tools).

## Selection

**Select** selects guides with a lasso or a click: Shift toggles, Ctrl deselects, Ctrl+Shift adds.
Its settings include a **Soft radius** for soft selection. **Edit Guides** has **Mirror
Selection** and **Clear Selection**.

## Symmetry

- **Symmetric grooming:** turn on **Symmetry** (**Mirror** in the tool card's **Applies to** block, or **Alt+Shift+X**) and
  every stroke is mirrored. While it is on, a red line on the scalp shows the mirror plane (with
  **Topology**, the scalp's middle loop) for as long as a brush is on, the cursor anywhere.
- **Mirroring one side:** groom one side, then use **Edit Guides > Mirror > Group...** or **Mirror > Whole Groom
  +X → -X**. Sync also copies children, hand edits, locks and painted attributes.

## Parting

Pick **Part** and draw along the parting on the scalp. Interpolation no longer blends guides
across the line, and a **Part** node opens the gap (its `width` sets how far). If the parting is
already combed into the guides, set the Part node's `partsFrom` to `auto` instead; it detects
the parts where the hair splits, with no drawing needed.

## Short hair without guides

For vellus hair, brows and short fur, the **Flow** brush grooms a direction field: drag arrows on
the scalp (drag over arrows to comb them, Ctrl to erase), or click to place whorls and other
singular points that hair turns around. A **Flow** node lays the strands along the field. See
[Fur & feathers](/docs/fur-and-feathers).

The arrows and sinks are kept on the groom itself, not in the Flow node, so they stay when you
delete or replace the node. The Flow tool's card counts them ("34 arrows · 2 sinks"). To remove
them all, click **Clear All** there, or use **GroomLab > Utilities > Delete All Flow Arrows**
(one undo step). To remove some, use **Erase** with **Flow** ticked, or Ctrl+drag with the Flow
brush.

## Scaling a groom

A groom scales with its scalp, like an object parented to it. Scale the head up 2x and the hair
is the same hair 2x as big: lengths, widths, clump sizes and the guides' shapes double, the
density drops to a quarter so the strand count stays the same, and curl frequency halves. The
stored values do not change; the Graph Editor shows the values at the size the groom was
groomed at.

- **The readout.** Attribute Editor > Inputs > **Groom Scale** shows how much the groom is scaled
  now: `1.00x` at the size it was groomed at. In the standalone app it is in the Display panel.
- **Scale Source** (Attribute Editor > Inputs; Display panel in the app;
  `groomlab.set_scale_source(groom, "area" | "transform" | "off")`) says what the scale follows:
  - **Surface Area** (the default): the scalp's surface area at the scene's start frame. Scaling
    the scalp's transform and **Freeze Transformations** give the same result (the groom does not
    jump when you freeze), and a scalp replaced by a higher or lower resolution version of the
    same surface stays at 1x. Editing or remodelling the mesh to another size scales the groom
    too; when that happens GroomLab says so once, in the Script Editor and in the view (the app:
    its status message): *"GroomLab: the scalp's area changed 12%: the groom scaled x1.06. Set
    Scale Reference to keep its sizes."*
  - **Transform Only**: the scale of the scalp's transform (and its rig's root joint) at the start
    frame, nothing else. Remodelling the mesh leaves the groom as it is. But Freeze
    Transformations resets the transform's scale to 1, so the groom goes back to 1x with it.
  - In short: **Surface Area survives Freeze Transformations; Transform Only survives
    remodelling.** In the standalone app (no transforms) Transform Only keeps the scale the groom
    opened with.
  - **Off**: sizes stay in scene units whatever the scalp's size, and a bigger scalp grows more
    strands at the same density.
- Animation after the start frame (a squash, blend shapes) does not change the scale. Nor does posing
  a rig at the start frame, when the groom has a rest mesh (a skinned scalp gets one): the scale
  is measured without the pose's stretch, so raising an arm keeps it, while scaling the rig
  scales the groom.
- **Rigs.** Scaling a rig at its root scales the skinned scalp, and the groom with it. Surface
  Area measures the skinned mesh, not its original shape; Transform Only reads the root joint's
  scale.
- **Set Scale Reference.** Makes the scalp's size now the groom's 1x (the button next to the
  readout, or `groomlab.set_scale_reference(groom)`), for both sources. Use it when you scaled or
  remodelled the scalp on purpose and want the groom to keep its sizes in scene units at the new
  size.
- **Transfers** keep the size shown by default; **Fit groom to the new scalp's size** in the
  Transfer window treats the new scalp as the old one resized, so the groom scales with it (see
  [Transfer to a new scalp](/docs/utilities#transfer-to-a-new-scalp)).
- **Presets and new nodes** are fitted to the size the groom is at 1x, so they match the values
  already in the graph.
- **Simulation.** Gravity and collision distances scale too, so a scaled groom moves like the
  unscaled one scaled. Changing the scale drops the simulated frames; a bake made at another
  scale no longer matches and is simulated again.
- **Groom files** keep the source and the reference sizes (`scaleSource`, `scaleReference`,
  `scaleTransformReference`), so exports, renders and the standalone app scale the groom the same
  way.
