Guides are the curves you shape by hand; the render strands are interpolated between them. The
basic workflow:

1. Open **GroomLab > Groom Tools**, pick **Place** and click or drag on the scalp.
2. Shape the guides with **Comb**, then refine them with the other Shape brushes.
3. Select some guides and choose **Edit Guides > Children** to generate child guides inside their
   tubes.
4. Continue brushing; the strands update with every stroke.

## The hierarchy

Primary guides are the top level. **Child guides** are generated inside each parent's **tube**
(the volume around a guide in which its children are placed), grandchildren inside the children's
tubes, to any depth. A child stores its shape relative to its parent, so editing a parent moves
and reshapes all its descendants.

- **Generate children:** select guides, set the count in the box beside **Edit Guides** (under
  **Guide groups & edits**) and choose **Edit Guides > Children**. Only the selected parents get
  children (with no selection, the group picked in the list). Select children and repeat for
  grandchildren.
- **Place children by hand:** pick a child group in **Group** (top of the Groom Tools) and use
  Place or Draw; each new child is parented to the nearest guide of the parent group.
- **Restructure:** **Reparent...** (move the selected children under another parent) and
  **Promote / Demote...** (move a group one level up, or under a sibling group) in Edit Guides
  change the hierarchy while keeping the guides' shapes.
- **Segments:** the slider under Guide groups & edits shows the point count of the selected guides,
  else of the picked group's (of every guide with All groups picked). Dragging it resamples them
  live; releasing applies it as one undo step. Frozen guides keep theirs. Resampling a whole group
  also sets the segments its new guides get from then on: drawn, placed, from curves, baked, and
  children generated into it (the group's tooltip shows it). Selected guides resampled get a count
  of their own: the group's count changing later leaves them as they are, until you resample them
  to the group's count again, or right-click the group > **Reset Segments** (every guide of the
  group back to its count, frozen ones kept). Give guides more segments before combing tight curls.
- **Which guides drive the strands:** by default Interpolate uses each group's deepest level.
  Where a group has children, the strands are interpolated from the children, and the primaries
  shape the strands through them; a group without children drives the strands itself ([Interpolate](/docs/node-graph#interpolate-and-guide-influence)).

## Tube guides

Every guide has a **tube**: the volume around it that its children are generated in and that
Interpolate's **Fill Tubes** fills with strands. Shaping tubes gives anime and cartoon hair, Pixar
style locks and spikes: each lock is shaped as a whole and its strands stay inside it to the tip,
with no mesh to model.

A tube is one **shape** carried along the guide, changed along the way by its **sections**:

- **Shape:** `round`, `ribbon` (a rounded rectangle), `teardrop` (round against the scalp, pointed
  away from it) or `blade` (pointed at both edges), or a **custom shape** from the groom's shape library
  (below). One per tube. Picking a shape for a guide lays its tube flat on the head: at twist 0 its
  width runs along the scalp, across the way the guide lies, and its height off it.
- **Width:** half the section's width, in scene units (a round tube's radius).
- **Aspect:** the section's height over its width. 1 draws the shape as designed; toward 0 it
  flattens into a ribbon.
- **Twist:** the angle the section is turned to at that point, in radians (not accumulated: 0 at
  the root and 3.14 at the tip turns it half a turn along the guide).

Width, aspect and twist are each a list of values spaced evenly from root to tip, blended between
(one value applies along the whole guide). The tube turns with the guide: its sections follow the
guide's frames, so bending a guide carries its tube and everything inside it.

**Sections follow the head.** A shaped tube's sections also turn to lie as the scalp does, all
along the guide: where the guide runs over the head (within a couple of section widths of the
scalp), twist 0 keeps the section's width along the scalp and its height off it, however the guide
curves round the head, so a ribbon combed over the head stays flat on it instead of drifting on
its side. Farther from the scalp the sections carry on as they left it (a lock hanging off the head
keeps its way), blending smoothly in between; twist turns the section from there. A plain round
tube is left as it is (its children and strands as before).

**Custom shapes (the shape library).** Each groom has a library of custom tube shapes, saved with
its guides (in the scene and in `.groom` files). A custom shape is an outline drawn by control
points in the section's square, -1 to 1 across and up with the guide through the middle: a smooth
curve through them (**Smooth**, the default) or straight edges with sharp corners. Guides take a
library shape by name just as they take a built-in one, and everything follows its outline exactly
as with a built-in: children are made inside it, Fill Tubes fills it, clumps stay in it, the wires
draw it. Editing a shape changes every guide using it at once (one undo step). An outline must not
cross itself and must surround its center (where the guide runs): edits that break either are
refused with a message saying what to fix. Saved points are fitted into the square, so a shape
drawn small comes back filling it. A guide whose library shape is missing (a hand-edited file)
loads round, with a warning.

**Children follow the tube.** Children are generated inside the shape's outline, not a disc, and
keep their place in its sections: flatten a tube and its children flatten with it, twist it and
they twist, taper it and they gather. A child's own tube is its parent's, narrower (**Tube scale**),
lying the same way. Children of a shaped tube whose guide leaves the head at a slant root on its
footprint (the root section laid flat on the scalp, as its strands do), spread over the section's
area rather than squeezed onto a line across it; guides within 30 degrees of standing straight up
keep their children's roots as before, and plain round tubes keep theirs.

**Setting up.** Fill Tubes on the Interpolate node is all it takes (the **Tube locks (anime)**
preset, under Hair in the Presets menu, has it on, with soft clumps that follow the tubes): place
guides, then shape their tubes with the Shape brush. A Clump after it follows the tubes by itself.

**Fill Tubes.** Turn on Interpolate's `fillTubes` and every strand rooted inside a guide's tube's
**footprint** (the root section laid flat on the scalp round the guide's root, so it covers the
section's whole area however steeply or shallowly the guide leaves the head) takes its place in the
tube's cross-section and keeps it from root to tip: the hair fills the tube exactly, flattening,
twisting and tapering with it, and is as long as the guide. Within a few widths of the root each
strand rises from its footprint into the tube. Strands rooted in no tube are interpolated from the guides
as usual, so tubes can cover the whole scalp or only some locks. The tubes of **every guide level**
fill, whatever level the Guides node hands Interpolate: where tubes of several levels hold a root
(a child's tube inside its parent's), the deepest one takes the strand, so the child's tube fills
its own footprint and the parent's tube the rest of its. A Guides node's `exclude` leaves its
groups' tubes out too. Where tubes of one level overlap, a strand takes the tube it lies most
centrally in. Strands in a tube carry `tubeId` (which tube), `tubeU` and `tubeV` (their place in
its section, -1 to 1).

**Tube Blend** (Interpolate's `tubeBlend`, 25% by default) is the tubes' soft edge, as a share of
each tube's half-width at its root. A strand rooted just outside a tube, within that distance of
its outline (measured on the footprint, equally thick all round however flat the tube),
leans from its usual shape toward riding the tube: all the way at the outline, none at the band's
edge, smoothly between. So there is no seam between the hair filling a tube and the hair around
it, nor round a child's tube inside its parent's. Strands past the band are exactly as without
Fill Tubes. 0 gives a hard edge.

**Clumps in tube space.** A Clump after it (with `followTubes`, on by default: nothing to set)
picks each tube's clumps among the tube's strands and clumps a strand only toward its own tube's
centers, so clumps stay inside the tube and flatten, twist and taper with it.

**New guides take the chosen shape.** A guide you place or draw (symmetry's mirror guide too) gets
the shape chosen in the **Tubes** panel (a built-in or a library shape; round until you pick one),
and, with **Match neighbors** on, its width, flatness and twist blended from the two nearest guides'
(the nearer counting more), so a new lock starts as thick and as flat as the locks round it. With no
guides around (or Match neighbors off), it takes the panel's **Width**, **Flatness** and **Twist**.
In Python: `brush_tool("place", tube={"shape": "leaf", "width": 0.1})` (and `brush_stroke(...,
tube=...)`); `placement_tube()` reads it back.

**Scatter fills tube footprints.** A Scatter with **guideRadius** keeps roots only near the guides;
round a guide with a shaped tube that means its footprint (the root section laid on the scalp, plus
**tubeMargin**, a share of the half-width as Tube Blend measures; 0 by default: exactly the footprint) instead of a disc round
its root, so a wide blade's or a ribbon's footprint fills evenly at the scatter's density, upright
or lying low. Guides without a shaped tube keep the disc of guideRadius.

**Shaping tubes in Maya.** Pick the **Shape** brush (**Groom Tools > Tools > Shape**). While it is
on, the tubes of every guide it can shape (shown and not frozen) draw as wires: the outline of each
section at every guide point, where the brush paints, and lines along the tube through the shape's
corners (a blade's points and crests, a ribbon's corners, a teardrop's point), so the shape reads.
The selected guides' tubes draw brighter.

- **Paints: Width, Flatness, Twist.** Drag over guides to paint their tubes near the brush, each
  section by the brush's falloff (and the **Root to tip** curve) at its guide point. **Width**
  grows the tubes (a negative strength shrinks them), **Flatness** flattens them toward a ribbon
  (negative: rounds them back), **Twist** turns them about the guide (negative: the other way). The first
  stroke on a tube gives it one section per guide point, so you shape it point by point.
  Symmetry, Selected only, frozen and soloed guides work as with every brush; each stroke is one
  undo step.
- **Shift+drag smooths** what the brush paints: the channel picked (width, flatness or twist) of the
  sections under the brush, each evened out toward its neighbours along the guide. The guides'
  shape stays as it is (other brushes' Shift+drag smooths the guides, as ever).
- **Tubes** (a section of its own in Groom Tools > Tools, opened by the Shape brush, Place and Draw, or from its header): each guide keeps its own tube, and the
  card edits **only its targets**: the selected guides (select them with **Select**), or with none
  selected the soloed ones (left unfrozen while the rest are frozen). It follows every change of the
  selection or solo. Pick the **Shape** (the built-ins, then the library's; it shows **Mixed** while
  the targets' shapes differ), set the **Width** (the tubes scaled so the first one's widest section
  is that wide; their taper and painted detail kept), **Flatness** and **Twist** (the same from root
  to tip). The shape and values are also what Place and Draw give new guides. **Smooth** evens out
  the targets' tubes along their guides, the **Current channel** (the one the brush paints) or **All
  channels**. **Fill Tubes** and **Tube Blend** set the groom's Interpolate nodes (disabled when the
  graph has none). Each change is one undo step.
- **The shape editor** (in the Tubes section): a square showing the chosen shape's outline, the guide
  at the cross, its height (off the scalp) up. A built-in shows read-only: **Edit as Custom** copies
  it into the library as a new shape (the targets take it). A library shape's points drag (the
  groom shows the change live while you drag; letting go saves it, one undo step, every guide using
  it following); **click the outline** between two points to add one; **right-click** a point to
  remove it (three stay at least). **Mirror** (on by default) keeps the left and right sides alike:
  a point and its mirror image move, come and go together, points on the middle line stay on it.
  **Smooth** switches between a smooth curve and straight edges. Under it: **New** (a circle to start
  from), **Rename** (type the name in place, Enter), **Duplicate**, **Delete** (click twice: the
  button says how many guides go round; they become round) and **Apply to Selected**. None of these
  opens a dialog.

**Seeing tubes.** Turn on **Display Tubes** in the groom's Attribute Editor (`displayTubes`; in
the app, **Tubes** in the Display panel) to draw every guide's tube with its shape and sections.

**From Python** (Maya), one undo step per call:

```python
import groomlab
# A flat ribbon that turns a quarter turn and tapers, on two guides.
groomlab.set_tube(groom, guides=ids, shape="ribbon", width=[0.4, 0.1], aspect=0.2, twist=[0.0, 1.57])
groomlab.set_tube(groom, group="primary", samples=8)  # 8 sections along each tube, to shape finely
groomlab.get_tube(groom, ids[0])  # {"shape": "ribbon", "width": [...], "aspect": [...], "twist": [...]}
groomlab.scale_tube(groom, 1.5, guides=ids)  # every width times 1.5, the taper kept
groomlab.smooth_tube(groom, guides=ids, channel="flatness", passes=3)  # or "width", "twist", "all" (None)
groomlab.set_fill_tubes(groom, on=True, blend=0.25)  # Fill Tubes and Tube Blend on every Interpolate
groomlab.fill_tubes(groom)  # {"fillTubes": True, "tubeBlend": 0.25}, or None without an Interpolate
groomlab.brush_stroke(groom, "shape", points, radius=0.3, tube_channel="flatness")  # a scripted stroke
# The shape library: custom outlines, (u, v) control points in the -1..1 square, in order round it.
groomlab.add_tube_shape(groom, "leaf", [(0, 1), (-0.6, 0.2), (0, -1), (0.6, 0.2)], smooth=True)
groomlab.set_tube(groom, guides=ids, shape="leaf")  # guides take it by name
groomlab.set_tube_shape_points(groom, "leaf", points)  # every guide using it follows (smooth= too)
groomlab.tube_shapes(groom)  # the built-ins and the library: name, builtin, points, smooth, users
groomlab.rename_tube_shape(groom, "leaf", "petal")
groomlab.remove_tube_shape(groom, "petal")  # its guides become round; returns how many
groomlab.preview_tube_shape(groom, "leaf", points)  # shown live, nothing stored (end_guide_preview)
groomlab.tube_targets(groom)  # what the Tubes panel edits: the selected guides, else the soloed ones
```

`set_tube` takes `guides`, `group` or `level` (default: the selected guides) and any of `width`,
`aspect`, `twist`, `shape` and `samples`; what you leave out stays. The command underneath is
`groomLabGuides -setTube <json>` (its keys: `shape`, `width`, `aspect`, `twist`, `samples`,
`scaleWidth`, and `smooth` (`width`, `aspect`, `twist` or `all`) with `passes`) and `-tube <id>`;
the library is `-tubeShapes` (a query, JSON), `-editTubeShape <json>` (`{"op": "add" | "set" |
"rename" | "remove", "name", "points", "smooth", "to"}`, one undo step) and `-previewTubeShape
<json>` (live, no undo step), and `-tubeTargets` gives the panel's targets. The brush takes
`-placeTube <json>` (Place and Draw) and `tube_channel` (`width`, `flatness`, `twist`;
`-tubeChannel` on `groomLabBrushCtx` and `groomLabBrushStroke`). Fill Tubes and tube-space clumps
run on the CPU; the GPU preview hands those nodes to it. The Tubes section, the shape editor
and the wires are Maya's for now; the app and Blender paint width with it and keep (and fill) a
groom's custom shapes, without an editor yet.

## Guide groups

Guides belong to named **guide groups**. A group is either a top-level group of primary guides
(`primary` by default, `braids`...) or a child group whose guides are children of the guides in
its parent group. Groups are listed in **Groom Tools > Tools > Guide groups & edits**, a tree with
the columns **Group**, a color swatch, **Guides** (count) and **Level**.

| Action | How |
| --- | --- |
| Create a group | **+ New Group**: a name and a parent (**None** for a top-level group, or a parent group for a child group). |
| Make it the active group | Click its row, or pick it in the **Group** menu at the top of the Groom Tools. Brushes only edit the active group's guides, and Place and Draw add guides to it. **All groups** brushes every guide. **Page Up** / **Page Down** step one level up or down. |
| Rename | Double-click the name, or **rename**. Guides node params that name the group are updated. |
| Show / hide | The row's checkbox. The guides' eye at the top of the Groom Tools hides or shows every group of the groom at once ([The current groom](#the-current-groom-and-what-shows)). |
| Color | The swatch shows the color the group draws in: filled when it has a color of its own, dashed around its automatic one. Click it to pick a color; right-click it (or **Default Color**) to clear. Every group without its own color, child groups too, gets a hue of its own, well apart from its parent's and its siblings'. To see a painted guide layer instead, set **Guide Color** (Display > Guides) to **Painted Layer**. The automatic colors start from a hue of the groom's own, from its name (the Maya transform, the Blender object, the app's file name), so two grooms side by side differ; the same name gives the same colors in Maya, Blender and the app. |
| Select its guides | **select**, or right-click > **Select Guides**. |
| Delete | **delete**: the group, its child groups and their guides. The dialog lists graph nodes that use the group. |
| Mirror | **Edit Guides > Mirror Group...** copies a top-level group to the other side. |

**Isolate**, next to the Group menu, draws the active group and its child groups at full color and
dims every other group.

**Hidden groups** are not drawn, not affected by brushes and not picked by the Select tool. Hiding
does not change the groom: the guides still drive their strands. Hiding applies to the named group
only, not to its child groups.

In the graph, groups are referenced by name: a **Guides** node's `group` and `exclude` params
choose which groups feed Interpolate, and a **Group** node with `by` `guideGroup` makes a strand
mask from the strands nearest a group's guides ([Restricting a node](/docs/node-graph#restricting-a-node-to-some-strands)).
Groups, and the guides in them, are saved with the scene and in `.groom` files; hidden state and
group colors are stored on the Maya node (`hiddenGuideGroups`, `guideGroupColors`) and not in the
`.groom` file.

```python
groomlab.list_guide_groups(groom)                      # [{name, parent, depth, count}, ...]
groomlab.add_guide_group(groom, "bangs", parent=None)
groomlab.rename_guide_group(groom, "bangs", "fringe")
groomlab.show_guide_group(groom, "primary", visible=False)
groomlab.set_guide_group_color(groom, "fringe", (1.0, 0.5, 0.2))
groomlab.select_guides(groom, group="fringe")
groomlab.guide_ids(groom, "fringe")                    # the group's guide IDs
```

A guide's group is set when it is created (the active group for Place and Draw, the child group
for Edit Guides > Children). There is no command to move existing guides to another group.

## Freeze and solo

Freezing protects guides from brushes. The freeze state is a per-guide value from 0 to 1, the
**freeze mask**; brushes scale their effect on each guide by 1 minus its freeze value, so a
partial value gives a partial effect. Child guides inherit their parent's freeze value. Frozen
guides are drawn blue. Freezing does not affect the strands.

- **Freeze** (a brush in the Guides category) paints the freeze mask; **Ctrl** unfreezes.
  **Unfreeze All**, next to it, clears the mask on every guide.
- **Ctrl+click** with any guide brush adds the guides inside the brush ring to the selection:
  every guide not selected freezes. **Ctrl+drag** paints guides into the selection with the ring,
  **Ctrl+Shift+drag** lassos them. **Ctrl+click** where the ring holds no guide, or a
  **Ctrl+Shift** lasso round none, deselects and unfreezes all.
- **Select** works the same way: the selected guides stay live, all others are frozen, and a
  click on nothing unfreezes all.
- **Solo new guides** (Place and Draw settings, off by default): each guide you place stays live
  and all others are frozen, so you can shape it immediately.
- With **Symmetry** on, the mirrored guides are soloed too. Each of these is one undo step.
- **Freeze exact guides:** the freeze mask is a guide map named `freeze`. Select guides, then in
  the **Paint** tab pick `freeze`, set **Value** to 1 and click **Fill**, which fills the selected
  guides only. From Python: `groomlab.freeze_guides(groom, guides)` (the selected guides by
  default; `value=0` unfreezes) and `groomlab.unfreeze_all(groom)`.

To freeze a whole group, select its guides (**select** in the group list) and fill `freeze`, or
in Python `groomlab.freeze_guides(groom, groomlab.guide_ids(groom, "fringe"))`.

### Detailing at higher density

A typical way to work from coarse to fine:

1. Groom the primary guides with the `primary` group active, until the overall shape is right.
2. Generate children (**Edit Guides > Children**), then make the child group active. Brushes now
   only affect the children, and the primaries stay as they are.
3. Hide the primary group if its curves get in the way, or turn on **Isolate** to dim it.
4. Solo areas while detailing: Ctrl+drag over the guides to work on (or Ctrl+Shift+drag a lasso
   round them), and everything else is frozen. Ctrl+click on empty space to release them.
5. Editing a primary later still carries its children, with their detail, along with it.

For more density than hand-placed children, interpolate dense guides procedurally with a
**ToGuides** node and edit them with a **Sculpt** node, whose edits survive upstream changes
([Other main nodes](/docs/node-graph#other-main-nodes)).

## Brushes

Select the groom and pick a brush. The radius is in screen pixels, strokes act in screen space,
and each drag is one undo step. The brushes are the same, in the same order, in Maya, the
[app](/docs/app) and [Blender](/docs/blender).

| Category | Brush | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Create | **Place** | Click for one guide, drag to place several (at least **Spacing** apart). New guides take the shape of the nearby guides (**Match nearby guides**). |
| | **Draw** | Press on the scalp and draw the guide's shape. |
| | **Part** | Draw a part line; interpolation does not blend guides across it. |
| | **Flow** | Draw direction arrows and whorls for short hair without guides (see below). |
| Shape | **Comb** | The main shaping brush: drags guides along the stroke with roots pinned. With **Stretch** on, combing also lengthens or shortens them. |
| | **Slide** | Moves roots along the scalp; the guides keep their shape. |
| | **Smooth**, **Straighten** | Remove kinks; relax guides toward a straight line. |
| | **Lift**, **Twist**, **Curl** | Raise guides off the scalp, rotate them around their root, curl them toward the tip. |
| | **Attract** | Pulls tips together (negative strength pushes them apart). |
| | **Direction** | Rotates guides about their roots toward the stroke direction, keeping lengths. |
| | **Noise** | Adds smooth random displacement that builds up with each stroke (a negative strength smooths it out). |
| | **Shape** | Paints guides' [tubes](#tube-guides) near the brush: **Width**, **Flatness** or **Twist** (a negative strength: the opposite), and sets the selected guides' tube shape, Fill Tubes and Tube Blend. |
| Trim | **Cut**, **Length**, **Erase** | Cut guides where the brush touches; lengthen or shorten them uniformly; delete guides, part lines or flow arrows (not the scattered hair: see below). |
| Paint (in the Paint tab) | **Paint** | Paints the map picked in the Paint tab, including the maps that drive settings such as density and width (see below, and [Painting maps](/docs/painting-maps)). |
| Guides | **Select**, **Freeze** | Select guides; paint the freeze mask. **Unfreeze All** is next to them. |

**Painting a setting** (density, width, length, clumping...). In the Paint tab, click **+ New Map**
and set **For** to the setting: **Density**, **Width**, **Length**, **Clump strength**, **Curl**,
**Noise amplitude**, **Flyaways**, or **Other setting...** for any other mappable parameter. GroomLab
creates a white map (so nothing changes yet), connects it to the setting, selects it and starts the
Paint brush on it, all in one undo step. If the setting already reads a map, that map is selected
instead. A stroke paints toward the **Value** (1 is the setting's full value); **Ctrl** paints
toward 0: thinner hair on the width map, no hair on the density map. For example, to thin the
sideburns, create the map for Density and Ctrl+paint over them.

**Erasing hair.** The **Erase** brush removes guides (with their children), part lines and flow
arrows, so it does nothing on a groom whose hair comes from the Scatter alone. To take hair away,
paint the density map holding **Ctrl** (Paint tab > **+ New Map** > For: **Density**); paint it
back without Ctrl.

### Keys

- **Ctrl** selects guides with the guide brushes (below). Painting maps or the freeze mask it
  does the opposite (Ctrl+Paint on the density map removes hair, Ctrl+Freeze unfreezes). A guide
  brush's opposite is its negative strength (Length shortens, Lift lays guides down).
- **Alt+Shift+X** turns symmetry on or off, after **GroomLab > Install GroomLab Hotkeys**.
- **Shift+drag** smooths, with any brush.
- **B** + drag sets the radius, **M** + drag the strength, after **GroomLab > Install GroomLab
  Hotkeys** (or middle-drag and Shift+middle-drag).
- **Ctrl+drag** paints the selection with the brush ring, without leaving the brush: every guide
  the ring passes over is selected as you go, and when you let go the guides not selected freeze,
  so the brush then works on the selection. **Ctrl+Shift+drag** lassos the selection instead.
  **Ctrl+click** adds the guides inside the ring; on none it deselects and unfreezes all (see
  [Freeze and solo](#freeze-and-solo)).
- **Page Up** and **Page Down** step the active group up or down a level.
- **Y** + click opens a marking menu, after **GroomLab > Install GroomLab Hotkeys**.
- Tablet **pen pressure** scales the strength.

**GroomLab > Help > Keyboard Shortcuts** lists every key (the same table as the app and Blender) and
marks the ones that need the GroomLab hotkey set. **Install GroomLab Hotkeys** shows which keys it
changed and the hotkey set it switched from.

## The current groom and what shows

One groom is **current**: the Groom Tools, the Graph Editor and every brush work on it.

- **Pick it** in the list at the top of the Groom Tools or in the Graph Editor's groom list: both
  move together. **Selecting a groom** in the viewport or the Outliner makes it current too. A
  picked groom stays current while you select a scalp, curves or anything else.
- **Pin the Graph Editor** (the pin beside its groom list) to keep it on its groom while the Groom
  Tools move on to another one; its list then changes only the graph. Unpin it and it shows the
  current groom again. The pin lasts for the session.
- **The eyes** beside the list: the first shows or hides the **hair** (the strands, and whatever
  the groom grows on them: instances, scales, cards, strand meshes; the groom's **Display Hair**),
  the second the **guides** (every guide group at once, with their tubes, dots, selection, parts
  and flow arrows; the groom's **Display Guides**). Each leaves the other as it is: hide the hair
  to see the guides alone, the guides to judge the hair. Both are viewport switches: renders and
  exports always have the hair. **Shift+click** an eye, or right-click it for **Show All** /
  **Hide All**, to do it for every groom in the scene.
- **GroomLab > Display** has **Show All Hair**, **Hide All Hair**, **Show All Guides** and
  **Hide All Guides**. With the GroomLab hotkey set, **Alt+Shift+G** turns every groom's guides
  off, and on again. (To hide a whole groom, hide its node as any other: **Ctrl+H**.)

```python
groomlab.set_current_groom(groom)        # what a pick in either list does
groomlab.active_groom()                  # the current groom
groomlab.set_guides_visible(groom, False)
groomlab.set_all_guides_visible(True)    # every groom's (GroomLab > Display)
groomlab.set_hair_visible(groom, False)  # the strands alone (displayHair)
groomlab.set_all_hair_visible(True)
```

<Callout title="In the app and in Blender">
  **App:** one groom per window, so the list is its name; the hair's eye and the guides' eye work
  the same (the hair goes and comes back at once, without evaluating), and **View > Guides On/Off**
  (**Alt+Shift+G**) and **GroomLab > Display** show or hide the guides.

  **Blender:** the groom at the top of the Groom Tools is the Graph Editor's too, and making a
  groom's scalp the active object makes it current. The eyes are the same (Shift+click or
  right-click: every groom): the hair's eye hides the hair object in the viewport (its render
  visibility stays), the guides' eye the guides; the node editor's pin keeps a Graph Editor on its
  groom.
</Callout>

## Brush settings

The active brush's settings are shown below the tabs; each brush shows only the settings it uses.
The sections under them follow the tool too: **Tubes** shows with Place, Draw, Shape and Select,
**While sculpting** with the brushes that stroke guides, and **Guide groups & edits** on the Tools
tab. **Output** is always there. Pick a tool to see its sections; with no tool picked, the window
shows the tiles, the guide groups and Output.

| Setting | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Radius**, **Strength** | Brush radius (pixels) and the amount applied per dab. Negative strength reverses the brushes that work in both directions. |
| **Falloff** | How the effect fades toward the brush edge: 0 hard, 1 soft from the center out. |
| **Falloff curve** | The falloff as a curve, center (left) to edge (right). Right-click it for presets. |
| **Root to tip** | How much of the strength each part of a guide receives, root (left) to tip (right); for example, style the ends and leave the roots. |
| **Symmetry** | In the **Group** row at the top of the Groom Tools, with or without a brush. Mirrors every stroke: across world **X**, or with **Topology** across the scalp's own left and right (for a head that is rotated, animated or not quite symmetric). |
| **Selected only** | Brush only the selected guides, weighted by the soft selection. |
| **Keep off scalp** | Guides never go below the scalp; points pushed under it slide along it. |
| **Front facing only** | A stroke does not reach what is on the far side of the head from the camera: guides, parts, flow arrows or paint. |
| **Acts on** | What a shaping brush moves: guides, part lines, flow arrows. |

## Selection

**Select** selects guides with a lasso or a click: Shift toggles, Ctrl deselects, Ctrl+Shift adds.
Its settings include a **Soft radius** for soft selection. **Edit Guides** has **Mirror
Selection** and **Clear Selection**.

## Symmetry

- **Symmetric grooming:** turn on **Symmetry** (in the Groom Tools' **Group** row, or **Alt+Shift+X**) and
  every stroke is mirrored. While it is on, a red line on the scalp shows the mirror plane (with
  **Topology**, the scalp's middle loop) for as long as a brush is on, the cursor anywhere.
- **Mirroring one side:** groom one side, then use **Edit Guides > Mirror Group...** or **Sync
  +X → -X**. Sync also copies children, hand edits, locks and painted attributes.

## Parting

Pick **Part** and draw along the parting on the scalp. Interpolation no longer blends guides
across the line, and a **Part** node opens the gap (its `width` sets how far). If the parting is
already combed into the guides, set the Part node's `partsFrom` to `auto` instead; it detects
the parts where the hair splits, with no drawing needed.

## Short hair without guides

For vellus hair, brows and short fur, the **Flow** brush grooms a direction field: drag arrows on
the scalp (drag over arrows to comb them, Ctrl to erase), or click to place whorls and other
singular points that hair turns around. A **Flow** node lays the strands along the field. See
[Fur & feathers](/docs/fur-and-feathers).

The arrows and sinks are kept on the groom itself, not in the Flow node, so they stay when you
delete or replace the node. The Flow tool's card counts them ("34 arrows · 2 sinks"). To remove
them all, click **Clear All** there, or use **GroomLab > Utilities > Delete All Flow Arrows**
(one undo step). To remove some, use **Erase** with **Flow** ticked, or Ctrl+drag with the Flow
brush.

## Scaling a groom

A groom scales with its scalp, like an object parented to it. Scale the head up 2x and the hair
is the same hair 2x as big: lengths, widths, clump sizes and the guides' shapes double, the
density drops to a quarter so the strand count stays the same, and curl frequency halves. The
stored values do not change; the Graph Editor shows the values at the size the groom was
groomed at.

- **The readout.** Attribute Editor > Inputs > **Groom Scale** shows how much the groom is scaled
  now: `1.00x` at the size it was groomed at. In the standalone app it is in the Display panel.
- **Scale Source** (Attribute Editor > Inputs; Display panel in the app;
  `groomlab.set_scale_source(groom, "area" | "transform" | "off")`) says what the scale follows:
  - **Surface Area** (the default): the scalp's surface area at the scene's start frame. Scaling
    the scalp's transform and **Freeze Transformations** give the same result (the groom does not
    jump when you freeze), and a scalp replaced by a higher or lower resolution version of the
    same surface stays at 1x. Editing or remodelling the mesh to another size scales the groom
    too; when that happens GroomLab says so once, in the Script Editor and in the view (the app:
    its status message): *"GroomLab: the scalp's area changed 12%: the groom scaled x1.06. Set
    Scale Reference to keep its sizes."*
  - **Transform Only**: the scale of the scalp's transform (and its rig's root joint) at the start
    frame, nothing else. Remodelling the mesh leaves the groom as it is. But Freeze
    Transformations resets the transform's scale to 1, so the groom goes back to 1x with it.
  - In short: **Surface Area survives Freeze Transformations; Transform Only survives
    remodelling.** In the standalone app (no transforms) Transform Only keeps the scale the groom
    opened with.
  - **Off**: sizes stay in scene units whatever the scalp's size, and a bigger scalp grows more
    strands at the same density.
- Animation after the start frame (a squash, blend shapes) does not change the scale. Nor does posing
  a rig at the start frame, when the groom has a rest mesh (a skinned scalp gets one): the scale
  is measured without the pose's stretch, so raising an arm keeps it, while scaling the rig
  scales the groom.
- **Rigs.** Scaling a rig at its root scales the skinned scalp, and the groom with it. Surface
  Area measures the skinned mesh, not its original shape; Transform Only reads the root joint's
  scale.
- **Set Scale Reference.** Makes the scalp's size now the groom's 1x (the button next to the
  readout, or `groomlab.set_scale_reference(groom)`), for both sources. Use it when you scaled or
  remodelled the scalp on purpose and want the groom to keep its sizes in scene units at the new
  size.
- **Transfers** keep the size shown by default; **Fit groom to the new scalp's size** in the
  Transfer window treats the new scalp as the old one resized, so the groom scales with it (see
  [Transfer to a new scalp](/docs/utilities#transfer-to-a-new-scalp)).
- **Presets and new nodes** are fitted to the size the groom is at 1x, so they match the values
  already in the graph.
- **Simulation.** Gravity and collision distances scale too, so a scaled groom moves like the
  unscaled one scaled. Changing the scale drops the simulated frames; a bake made at another
  scale no longer matches and is simulated again.
- **Groom files** keep the source and the reference sizes (`scaleSource`, `scaleReference`,
  `scaleTransformReference`), so exports, renders and the standalone app scale the groom the same
  way.
