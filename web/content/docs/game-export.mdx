## Cards

Add a **Cards** node (Add Node > Games) at the point in the graph that should be converted to
cards, or run `groomlab.create_cards(groom, lod="mid")`. Cards are placed along the groom's clumps
and shape, and their textures are baked from the groom's own strands.

The top of the node's panel shows the cards' cost and has **Show cards**, **Show textures**,
**LOD**, **Bake Maps**, **Bake Scalp** and the exports, in view however far its parameters scroll.
While a bake runs, the panel shows a wait cursor, greys out its buttons and says what it is
baking.

- **LODs:** Hero, Mid and Low set a triangle budget (about 50k, 15k and 4k) and a matching card
  layout. Lower levels use fewer cards of the same size, so the silhouette is preserved.
- **Shading consistency:** in the viewport, cards are lit like the strands (normals, highlight
  and ambient), and each card is tinted with its own strands' color, root to tip, while the
  textures stay shared across cards. The exports carry the same data: the normals, and the tint as
  a vertex color (glTF: `_TINT`).
- **Baked maps:** depth, coverage, tangent, attribute, color and AO, in Unreal's hair card texture
  layout.
- **Stable atlas:** after a bake, cards rebuilt by later edits keep their place in the atlas. A
  node whose maps are out of date shows an orange badge.
- **Bake Scalp** bakes the hair near the skin into the scalp's UVs, with a hair cap mesh, so low
  LODs don't show bare skin between cards.
- Cards are always built from the full groom; the viewport preview density does not affect them.

## Export card maps

**Export Maps...** on the Cards node (or **GroomLab > Export > Card Maps...**) writes the cards'
texture maps the way your engine wants them, in one click. In the app it is the **Card maps**
target of the Export panel; in Blender, **Card Maps...** in the GroomLab panel.

1. Pick a **Preset**: **Unreal Hair Cards**, **Unreal (packed)**, **Unity HDRP Hair**,
   **Separate maps (all)**, or one of your own.
2. Set the **Resolution** (any size up to 8192; the atlas is square), the **Format** (TGA, PNG,
   TIFF or EXR) and the **Bit depth** (8 or 16 for PNG and TIFF; 16-bit half or 32-bit float for
   EXR; TIFF also 32-bit float).
3. Leave **Padding** on Auto (8 texels at 2K and below, scaled up at higher resolutions): colors
   spread past the strands so mip maps don't bleed black into the edges. **Supersampling** 2x or 4x
   bakes the maps larger and scales them down for smoother edges (slower).
4. Choose the folder and the name, and click **Export**. Thumbnails of the written maps appear
   under the button.

The Unreal Hair Cards preset as 8-bit TGA writes the same files as **Bake Maps**, byte for byte.
Exporting never changes the groom's own bake (what its cards view shows).

### The maps

| Map | What it is | Channels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `depth` | How far the strands lie off the card: 0.5 on the card, higher in front (Unreal's Depth; FiberShop's Height) | 1 |
| `coverage` | Opacity (the alpha): how much of each texel the strands cover | 1 |
| `tangent` | The strands' direction in card space as Unreal reads it: x across, y root to tip, z out of the card | 3 |
| `flow` | The same direction as a two-channel flow map in the texture (x across, y root to tip up the texture) | 2 |
| `normal` | A tangent-space normal of the strands as round fibers: facing out of the card at a strand's center and turning toward its sides at the edges. OpenGL or DirectX green, set per preset | 3 |
| `color` | The strands' color (albedo) | 3 |
| `ao` | Ambient occlusion: 1 where the hair is open, darker under other hair | 1 |
| `root` | 0 at each strand's root, 1 at its tip (Unreal's CoordU) | 1 |
| `seed` | A random value per strand (Unreal's Seed) | 1 |
| `id` | A random value per clump (the strands' clump; per card without one) | 1 |
| `rootuv` | Where each strand grows on the scalp: its UV there | 2 |
| `specular` | Shine: brightest at the root and about 40% duller at the tip, varied per strand by ±15% and dimmed where occluded | 1 |
| `translucency` | How much hair the light passes through: the strands over each texel, softened over a few texels. Higher where the hair is denser, so it works as a thickness map; invert it for a transmission mask | 1 |
| `iridescence` | A FeatherColor's iridescence (0 without one) | 1 |

Every data value is written 0 to 1 in every format (a direction as 0.5 + 0.5 × its component), so a
material reads PNG, TGA, TIFF and EXR files the same way. Only color depends on the file's color
space: **Auto** writes it sRGB in TGA, PNG and TIFF and linear in EXR.

### Presets

| Preset | Files (R, G, B, A) |
| --- | --- |
| **Unreal Hair Cards** (TGA) | `Depth`, `Coverage`, `Tangent` (xyz), `Attribute` (root U, root V, seed, root to tip), `Color` (RGB, plus the iridescence in alpha when the groom has any), `AO` |
| **Unreal (packed)** (TGA) | `Color` (RGB + AO in alpha), `Data` (depth, root to tip, seed, coverage), `Tangent` (xyz) |
| **Unity HDRP Hair** (PNG) | `BaseColor` (RGB + coverage), `Normal` (OpenGL), `Mask` (0, AO, root to tip, specular as smoothness), `Tangent` (xyz), `Data` (depth, ID, translucency) |
| **Separate maps (all)** (PNG) | Every map in its own file: `Albedo`, `Alpha`, `Depth`, `Direction` (flow), `Tangent`, `Normal`, `Root`, `ID`, `Seed`, `AO`, `Specular`, `Translucency`, `RootUV`, and `Iridescence` when the groom has any |

The Unreal presets use DirectX normals; Unity and Separate maps use OpenGL normals.

### Packing your own

Open **Advanced** to see the packing table: one row per output file, with its name (the `{map}` in
the file name), four channels R, G, B and A, and a color space. Each channel takes a map's channel
(`depth`, `tangent.x`, `color.r`...), a constant **0** or **1**, or nothing (a file with only R is
grayscale; with A it is RGBA). Check **inv** to write 1 minus the value. **Add File** and
**Remove File** change the rows. Any edit switches the preset to **Custom**.

Type a name beside **Save as Preset** to keep it. Presets are JSON files in
`Documents/GroomLab/card_presets`, shared by Maya, the app and Blender; send the file to share
one. Folders listed in `GROOMLAB_CARD_PRESET_PATH` are read too (for a studio's shared presets).
**Delete** removes a preset of yours. **File names** sets the naming template, with `{name}`,
`{map}` and `{res}` (default `{name}_{map}`).

```json
{
  "name": "My packed",
  "format": "png",
  "bitDepth": 8,
  "normal": "directx",
  "files": [
    {"map": "Mask", "channels": ["depth", "root", "id", "coverage"]},
    {"map": "Color", "channels": ["color.r", "color.g", "color.b", "1-ao"], "colorSpace": "srgb"}
  ]
}
```

A channel name ending in `?` (`iridescence?`) is left out when the groom doesn't have that data.

```python
groomlab.bake_card_maps(groom, preset="Unity HDRP Hair", resolution=4096, format="png", bit_depth=16,
                        directory="C:/game/hair", name="hair")
groomlab.card_map_presets()        # every preset, as dicts
groomlab.card_map_sources()        # the maps and their channels
groomlab.save_card_map_preset("My packed", preset_dict)
```

### In the engine

- **Unreal:** import the maps with **sRGB** on only for color (the Color map, and the Color of
  the packed preset, whose alpha stays linear). Set the data maps (Depth, Coverage, Tangent,
  Attribute, AO, Data) to sRGB off with the **Masks** or **Linear Color** compression. With
  Unreal Hair Cards, set the textures on the groom asset's Cards LOD; the Hair Attributes node
  reads them. Unreal (packed) is for a hair card material of your own (Hair shading model, Masked).
- **Unity HDRP:** import BaseColor as sRGB with alpha as transparency; set Normal to **Normal
  map** (OpenGL, Unity's convention); set Mask, Tangent and Data with **sRGB (Color Texture)**
  off. Mask follows the Lit mask map layout, so its G (AO) and A (smoothness) work with the Lit
  and Hair shader graphs as they are.
- **Bit depth:** 8-bit is enough for color, coverage and AO. Use 16-bit PNG or EXR for depth and
  directions on hero assets to avoid banding.

## Export targets

| Target | Menu | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Unreal** cards | Cards node > Export Unreal Cards... (or GroomLab > Export > Unreal Cards...) | `_cards.fbx`, the seven maps, the hair cap and a JSON describing the channels |
| **Unreal** strands | GroomLab > Export > Unreal Groom... | Alembic in Unreal's groom schema, with guides for simulation |
| **Unity** cards | GroomLab > Export > Unity Cards... | FBX (Y up, meters), PNG maps and suggested HDRP and URP materials |
| **Unity** strands | GroomLab > Export > Unity Strands (.abc)... | Alembic for Unity's strand hair package |
| **glTF** cards | GroomLab > Export > glTF Cards... | `.glb` with embedded maps, one mesh per LOD |
| Card maps | Cards node > Export Maps... (or GroomLab > Export > Card Maps...) | The maps as a preset packs them: TGA, PNG, TIFF or EXR, up to 8K ([Export card maps](#export-card-maps)) |

When several LODs are exported into one folder, file names end in `_hero`, `_mid` or `_low`.

```python
groomlab.create_cards(groom, lod="hero")
groomlab.export_cards_unreal(groom, "C:/game/hair", "hair", lod="hero")
groomlab.export_cards_gltf(groom, lods=["hero", "mid", "low"])
groomlab.export_unreal_groom(groom, "C:/game/hair.abc")
```

### Importing the groom into Unreal

Import the `.abc` in Unreal as a Groom with the **default conversion settings** (Rotation 0, 0, 0;
Scale 1, 1, 1). No rotation or flip is needed: the export writes the strands in Unreal's own
space (Z up, left-handed, centimeters), turned from your scene's the way Unreal's FBX importer
turns a mesh from the same scene (a Y-up scene's (x, y, z) becomes (x, z, y), a Z-up one's
(x, -y, z); Blender's meters become centimeters). So the groom lines up with the head imported as
FBX from the same scene, with that importer's defaults too.

For hair that follows the character, import the head as a skeletal mesh, create a **Groom
Binding** from the groom to it, and set the binding on the Groom component. In Blender the groom
is in the scalp object's local space: apply the scalp's transforms (or leave it at the origin)
before exporting the head and the groom.

### Unreal groom attributes

The Unreal groom export writes the attributes Unreal's groom importer reads, as arbGeomParams:
`groom_version_major` / `groom_version_minor`, `groom_group_name`, `groom_group_id` (from the
`groupId` attribute), `groom_id`, `groom_width` (per point), `groom_root_uv` (with scalp UVs),
`groom_color` (per point, with a color node: the hair's appearance, what it looks like, see
[Base color and appearance](/docs/rendering#base-color-and-appearance)), `groom_roughness` (with HairColor's roughness),
`groom_clump_id` (with a Clump), `groom_iridescence` (with FeatherColor iridescence) and
`groom_guide` on the guide curves. It also writes the 64-bit strand ID as
`groomlab_strand_id_low` / `groomlab_strand_id_high`. Other GroomLab attributes, including
Attribute node output, are not written to the Unreal groom file.

## Color patterns in engines

Bands, FeatherColor patterns and HairColor tips are per-point colors, and FeatherColor's
iridescence is a per-point mask ([Color patterns](/docs/color-patterns)):

| Target | Colors | Iridescence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unreal strands | `groom_color` per vertex | `groom_iridescence` per vertex; with **Iridescence as AO** (`iridescence_as_ao=True`) also `groom_ao`, which the Hair Attributes material node exposes |
| Cards (Unreal, glTF, Unity) | Baked into the Color map as the hair's appearance (a feather's card is its vane laid flat, so its patterns map directly) | The Color map's alpha (Unreal; RGBA only then); Unity's Mask map red channel; glTF: not carried |
| Unity strands | One color per strand (`Cd`, as in GroomLab's Alembic; bands and patterns along a strand are not carried) | Not carried |
| glTF cards | The baked `_BaseColor` and each card's `_TINT` | Not carried (the alpha is the coverage) |

The repository's `docs/UNREAL_HAIR_MATERIAL.md` describes an Unreal material (for strands and
cards) that mixes in the iridescent color by that mask, with the hue rotated by the view angle;
`tools/unreal/groomlab_hair_material.json` describes the same graph node by node.

## Feathers in engines

- **As cards:** `groomlab.create_feather_cards(groom, lod="mid")` creates one alpha-tested strip
  per feather, with both vanes and the rachis baked into its texture, and similar feathers
  sharing a few textures. Each card is folded along a spine so lifted or cupped vanes form a V.
  Unreal, Unity and glTF export them.
- **As strands:** the rachis becomes a mesh to skin with the character, and the barbs are strands
  rooted on it.
  - Unreal: `groomlab.create_rachis_mesh`, `export_rachis` and `export_unreal_groom`.
  - Unity: **GroomLab > Export > Unity Feathers...** writes the strands, the rachis FBX and a
    manifest of the Hair Asset settings.
