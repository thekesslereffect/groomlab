GroomLab for Blender provides the same grooming workflow as the Maya plug-in: the same **Groom
Tools** (Place, Draw, Comb, Smooth, Cut, Paint, Select, Freeze...), guide groups and painted maps,
a **pie menu** on **Y** in place of Maya's marking menu, and GroomLab's Graph Editor with the same
node categories and presets as Maya and the GroomLab app. A groom saved from Blender evaluates to the same strands in
Maya, and the other way round.

It is a Blender **extension** for Blender 5.2 LTS on Windows (x64), distributed from our site.

## Install

<Steps>
<Step>

### Download the extension

[Download GroomLab for Blender](https://downloads.getgroomlab.com/blender/GroomLab-Blender-windows-x64-latest.zip).
Do not unzip it; Blender installs the zip directly.

</Step>
<Step>

### Install from Disk

In Blender choose **Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions**, open the **⌄** menu at the top right and
choose **Install from Disk...**, then pick the zip. GroomLab appears in the list, enabled.

</Step>
<Step>

### Open the GroomLab tab

In the 3D viewport press **N** for the sidebar and pick the **GroomLab** tab.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info" title="Updates">
  **GroomLab > Help > Check for Updates...** shows the newest version and what's new; **Update**
  downloads it and installs it over this one (restart Blender to finish). Once a day GroomLab also
  checks at startup and says so in the Groom Tools when a newer version is out (turn it off in the
  add-on's preferences). Both need Blender's **Allow Online Access** (Preferences > System >
  Network). You can also add `https://downloads.getgroomlab.com/blender/index.json` as a remote
  repository (**Get Extensions > Repositories > + > Add Remote Repository**) to get updates from
  Blender itself.

## Make a groom

1. Select the mesh the hair grows on (the scalp or the whole head, at real size: a head is about
   0.2 m), with a UV map.
2. Choose **GroomLab > Create Groom from Selection** in the top bar (or **Object > Create Groom
   from Selection**, or the same button in the Groom Tools). The groom starts from the default
   graph, fitted to the mesh's size, as in Maya and the app; place guides with the Groom Tools, and
   pick a look later from the Presets menu.

GroomLab adds a Curves object **`<mesh>_hair`** parented to the mesh. It holds the evaluated
strands, is not selectable, and is rewritten whenever the groom changes. The guides are not
Blender curves: they are GroomLab's guide hierarchy (as in Maya), drawn as an overlay and edited
with the Groom Tools.

## Groom Tools

The **GroomLab** tab of the 3D viewport's sidebar (**N**) opens on **Groom Tools**, the equivalent
of Maya's Groom Tools panel, with the same sections, names and order (Maya, Blender and the app
build their menus and panels from one set of definitions):

- At the top: the **groom** the tools work on (the Graph Editor's too; making a groom's scalp the
  active object makes it the groom), its eyes (show or hide the hair alone, in the viewport: renders
  keep it; and the guides alone: every guide group; **Shift+click** or right-click: every groom's),
  the **Graph Editor** and a **More** menu. **GroomLab > Display** shows or hides every groom's hair
  (**Show All Hair**, **Hide All Hair**) or every groom's guides. The pin in the Graph
  Editor's header keeps it on its groom while the Groom Tools move on.
- **Your first groom** walks you through placing guides and giving the groom a look.
- **Group**: the guide group the brushes edit and new guides go into (**All groups**: every
  guide); **Isolate** dims the other groups.
- **Tools**, **Paint**, **Correctives** and **Photos** tabs. The tools come by category, with Maya's icons:
  **Create** (Place, Draw, Part, Flow), **Shape** (Comb, Slide, Smooth, Straighten, Lift, Twist,
  Curl, Attract, Direction, Noise, Tube), **Trim** (Cut, Length, Erase), **Paint** (Paint) and
  **Guides** (Select, Freeze, and Unfreeze All beside them). Shift+drag with a
  guide brush paints guides into the selection (the rest freeze); Shift+click adds the guides in
  the brush ring, and where it holds none deselects and unfreezes them all. Ctrl does the brush's
  opposite; painting maps, Shift+drag smooths.
- The active tool's **card** shows what it does and exactly its settings, by Maya's names:
  **Radius** (pixels), **Strength** (±: tools that work both ways go negative), **Falloff**,
  **Falloff curve**, **Root to tip**, **Symmetry** (World X or Topology), **Selected only**, **Pen
  pressure**, **Keep off scalp**, **Front facing only**; Place and Draw's **New guides** (Match
  nearby guides, Length, Segments, Spacing, Solo new guides), Paint's map, Comb's
  **Stretch**, what the Shape brush **Paints** on the tubes (Width, Flatness or Twist), what
  shaping brushes act on (Guides, Parts, Flow), what Erase erases, the Part tool's next part name
  and its list of parts (**Remove**, **Clear All**), Flow's arrows or sinks (Whorl, Gather, Part
  out), how many arrows and sinks the groom keeps and **Clear All** (also **GroomLab > Utilities >
  Delete All Flow Arrows**), the Select tool's lasso or box and soft radius, the freeze mask. Each
  tool keeps its own strength.
- **Undo** / **Redo** name the groom's last edit.
- Folded below, in Maya's order: **Tubes** (with Place, Draw, Shape and Select), **While
  sculpting** (with the brushes that stroke; guides only while a stroke is live; full density),
  **Guide groups & edits** (Tools tab), **Output** (open: **Export** and **Import** menus) and
  **Display** (Blender's own).

The lists (guide groups, maps, correctives, photos) have Maya's action bar above them and Maya's
right-click menu, both on a right-click over an item and behind the arrow button beside the list.

### Guide groups & edits

**+ New Group**, then **Rename**, **Select** and **Delete** for the group picked in the list. The
list shows each group's guides, level, eye and, when it has one, its own color. Right-click a group
for **New Group...**, **Rename**, **Color...** (the group's viewport color), **Default Color** (back
to its level's), **Select Guides** and **Delete**.

**Edit Guides** has **Children** (the count beside it), **Sync +X → -X**, **Sync -X → +X**,
**Mirror Selection** (select the mirror images of the selected guides), **Mirror Group...** (a root
group to the other side), **Reparent...** (the selected children under another parent, by its
guide ID), **Promote / Demote...** and **Clear Selection**; then Blender's own Lock, Unlock, Freeze,
Unfreeze, Unfreeze All, Delete Selected and Select All. **Segments** resamples the selected guides
(else the picked group's, else every guide) along their smooth curve, frozen guides kept: the
viewport shows it as you drag, and it is applied as one undo step when you stop. A whole group
resampled keeps the count for its new guides; selected guides resampled keep a count of their own
when the group's changes (resample them to the group's count to follow it again, or right-click the
group > Reset Segments for all of them).

### Tubes

The **Tubes** section edits the tubes of the selected guides (else the guides left unfrozen while
the rest are frozen), and sets the tube Place and Draw give new guides:

- **Shape**: Round, Ribbon, Teardrop, Blade, then the groom's library of custom shapes (**Mixed**
  while the targets differ). Picking one gives it to the targets.
- **Edit as Custom** copies a built-in into the library. The library list adds (**+**), duplicates,
  renames and deletes shapes (deleted: its guides go round). **Apply to Selected** gives the
  targets the shape picked. (The outline editor is in Maya and the app.)
- **Width**, **Flatness** and **Twist**: the targets' tube, applied as one undo step when you stop
  dragging (Width scales the tube, its taper kept).
- **Smooth** evens out what the brush painted along each guide: the channel the Shape brush paints,
  or all three.
- **Fill Tubes** and **Tube Blend**: strands rooted in a guide's tube ride it to the tip (the
  graph's Interpolate nodes).

Pick a tool and drag over the groom in the viewport. The brush ring follows the cursor on the
scalp in Maya's colors (yellow; red for Part and Erase, orange for Flow, green for Place and Draw)
and highlights the guides it would affect. During a stroke the hair is drawn at a reduced density;
the full preview density returns on release.

### Keys while a tool is active

| Keys | |
|---|---|
| **Ctrl** + drag / click | With a guide brush: paint the selection / add the guides in the ring (the rest freeze; on none: unfreeze all) |
| **Ctrl+Shift** + drag | With a guide brush: lasso the selection (round none: deselect and unfreeze all) |
| **Ctrl** + painting | The opposite (Ctrl+Freeze unfreezes, Ctrl+Paint on the density map removes hair) |
| **Shift** + drag | Smooth instead |
| **Shift** + click | Solo the guide (the rest freeze) and brush its group; off a guide, unfreeze them all |
| **B** + drag, **M** + drag | The brush radius, the strength (Maya's hotkeys) |
| **S** | The Select tool (drag a lasso; Shift toggles, Ctrl deselects; click a guide) |
| **Page Up** / **Page Down** | The group a level up or down |
| **Y** | The pie menu |
| **Ctrl+Z** / **Ctrl+Shift+Z** | Undo / redo the groom's edits (strokes, guide edits, maps) |
| **Esc** or right-click | Cancel a live stroke; **Esc** again leaves the tool |

Navigation (middle mouse, wheel, numpad) works as always while a tool is active. Outside a
GroomLab tool, Ctrl+Z is Blender's own undo.

### The pie menu

**Y** in the viewport opens GroomLab's pie menu, laid out like Maya's marking menu: **Comb** at the top, **Slide**,
**Smooth**, **Cut**, **Place** at the bottom, **Draw**, **Length**, **Lift**, and below it the rest
of the tools, Select, Freeze and Groom Tools. Change its key in **Edit > Preferences > Add-ons >
GroomLab**.

## Paint maps

The **Paint** tab lists the groom's maps: 0-1 values (or colors) painted on the scalp that any
param can read through a Map node. **New Map** makes one on the guides, on the scalp's vertices,
or as a texture; **Import...** makes a texture map from an image. Pick it, take the **Paint** tool
and paint toward the **Value** (a color map: the **Color**, in the channels ticked); Ctrl paints
toward 0 (a color map: toward the inverse), Shift+drag smooths. **Fill** sets the map to the value
or the color everywhere (a guide map: on the selected guides when some are). **Show on** colors the
guides by a guide map, or shows the map on the scalp while painting. **Read by** lists the params
the map feeds.

The bar's icons rename, convert and delete the picked map. Right-click it for the whole menu:
**Rename...** (what reads it follows), **Convert to** (on the guides, on the scalp's vertices, or a
texture at a resolution you pick), **Make Gray** / **Make Color**, **Relink Image...**, **Reload
Image** (after editing it outside Blender), **Show in Explorer**, **Delete** and **Delete Its File
Too** (on by default: deleting a texture map deletes the image GroomLab made for it).

To paint a setting, set **New Map**'s **For** to **Density**, **Width**, **Length**, **Clump
strength**, **Curl**, **Noise amplitude**, **Flyaways** (the ones the graph has a node for), or
**Other setting...** for any mappable param, pick vertices or a texture, and click OK. A white map
is created, connected to the setting, selected and painted with the **Paint** tool, in one undo
step; if the setting already reads a map, that map is picked instead. Strokes paint toward the
**Value** (1: the setting's full value); **Ctrl** paints toward 0, so Ctrl on the density map
removes hair.

## Correctives

The **Correctives** tab is Maya's stack of sculpt layers: the correctives (top of the stack
first), the graph's **Sculpt** nodes and the **Base groom**. The row you pick is what the brushes
edit: the base groom's guides, a corrective on top of it, or a Sculpt node's guides.

- **+ New** adds a corrective on every frame; **◆ Fix Frame** adds one on this frame, keyed in
  over the 6 frames before and out over the 6 after. The bar also moves the picked corrective up
  or down, solos it and deletes it.
- **Weight** is the picked corrective's weight on this frame. Its keys are Blender keyframes: **◆**
  keys it here, changing a keyed weight keys it on the current frame, and you can move or edit the
  keys in the Timeline, Dope Sheet or Graph Editor. GroomLab samples the curve on every frame.
- Right-click a corrective for **Go to Its Frame**, **Key Weight Here**, **Clear Keys (every
  frame)**, **Rename**, **Solo** and **Delete**; right-click a Sculpt node for **Clear Edits**.

## Photos

The **Photos** tab grooms to reference photos, as Maya's does. Each photo is a Blender camera with
the photo as its background image, in a **GroomLab References** collection.

- **+ Import Photo...** makes the camera, in front of the groom and aimed at it. Rename and Remove
  are beside it (the photo file stays on disk); right-click the list for the same.
- **Look Through** shows the photo's camera in the viewport and sets the scene's render size to the
  photo's, so the camera frame has the photo's shape. Click it again to go back to your view.
  **Lock** keeps the camera where it is. **Opacity**, **Front** / **Back** (the photo over the mesh
  or behind it) and the eye set how the photo shows.
- **Align by Points** lines the camera up with the mesh. Turn on **Pick Points**, then click a
  feature on the photo while looking through its camera, and the same feature on the mesh in
  another view (the pairs fill in turn and draw numbered; **Esc** when done). **Solve** finds the
  camera's position, rotation and focal length from 6 pairs, or from 4 with **Keep focal length**.
  Each pair shows its error in pixels, and pairs that stand out are named so you can pick them
  again. Right-click a pair to pick its photo or mesh point again, or delete it.
- **Open Guides from Photos...** (also **GroomLab > Guides from Photos (experimental)...**) grows
  guides from every photo camera into a **fromReference** guide group, replaced each time. Brush
  them like any guides; GroomLab's **Undo** takes them back.

The photos are saved with the groom: a `.groom` file saved in Blender opens with its photos in the
GroomLab app, and a groom file with photos opened in Blender gets its cameras back. The flow arrows
and mask touch-ups drawn in Maya's or the app's Photos window are kept and used; drawing
them is Maya's and the app's.

## Viewport display

Guides are drawn as in Maya: each group in a color of its own (or by a painted layer), frozen
guides blue, the selection white with its soft-selection falloff orange, root and tip dots, tubes
when shown, part lines red, flow arrows orange. **Display** (under Groom Tools) has Maya's toggles: Guides,
Tubes, Flow, Strands, the guide points, the guide colors and the **Strand Colors** (Solid,
Color set, Attribute, Random, Clump, Density). Strand colors are a color attribute on the hair
(`groomlab_display`): **Show in Viewport** sets Solid shading's color to Attribute. Renders keep
the hair's material.

## Presets and the graph

**Presets** lists the shipped looks and your own (shared with Maya and the app) by category.
**Apply** replaces the groom's graph with the preset's, fitted to the size of your scalp (your
guides stay). **Insert After Node** adds a preset's look (frizz, flyaways, gravity...) after the
selected node, as one compound node.

**Graph** lists the groom's nodes, the strands' chain from the roots to the output first. Select a
node to edit its params below the list:

- Params come in groups (click a group's name to open it) with the rarely needed ones folded into
  **Advanced**; params that don't apply to the node's current mode are hidden.
- Lengths and widths show in the scene's units (Blender's own unit display), densities in hairs
  per cm², per-length rates per scene unit, fractions as sliders. GroomLab stores centimeters, as
  Maya does, so a groom means the same everywhere.
- Ramps and painted maps are shown here; edit ramps in the Graph Editor (below), paint maps in
  Maya or the GroomLab app.

**+** adds a node of the **New Node** type after the selected one, **−** removes the selected node
(the chain closes over it) and the arrows move it along the chain. For the whole graph, open the
Graph Editor.

## The Graph Editor

**Graph Editor** (in the GroomLab tab's Graph panel) opens the groom's graph in a node editor of
its own, as Maya's GroomLab Graph Editor shows it. Any node editor shows it too: set its type to
**GroomLab Graph** and pick the groom's graph (`<mesh> Graph`). Choosing another groom in the
editor's header (or the GroomLab tab) shows that groom's graph.

- **Nodes** carry their type's ports, colored by what flows through them as in Maya: strands
  orange, guides blue, meshes green, fields violet, curves yellow, strands-or-guides grey. A
  param exposed as an input is a diamond port. Each node is tinted with its category's color
  (Grow, Shape, Look, Color...) and shows a couple of the params you changed.
- **Add** a node with **Shift+A** or **Tab** (search), by the same categories as Maya and the app
  (Sources, Grow, Shape, Deform, Collide, Look...), each node's description as its tooltip. Drag from a
  port to wire it; a wire the graph can't take (a mesh into strands, a loop) is refused and the
  sidebar says why. **X** or **Delete** removes nodes, **Shift+D** or **Ctrl+D** duplicates them
  with their params, **F2** renames, **M** or **B** bypasses a node (its input passes straight
  through).
- **V** (Focus) shows the groom up to the selected node in the viewport instead of the output's;
  renders keep the output. On a Clump it shows its clump centers as dots on the scalp too. **Set as Output** makes a node the groom's output. **F** frames the
  selection, or everything.
- Every edit goes to the groom at once and the hair follows; **Ctrl+Z** undoes it, the graph too.
- The menus are Maya's, item for item: right-click a node for **Set as Output**, **Focus**,
  **Bypass**, **Evaluate** (Always, Viewport Only, Render Only), **Rename**, **Duplicate**,
  **Expose Param as Input**, a compound's **Ungroup**, **Show Inner Param**, **Save as Node...**
  and **Save to Library**, a script node's **Edit Node...**, **Group Selection into Compound**,
  **Add Backdrop Around Selection** and **Delete**. The header's **Edit** menu has Undo, Redo,
  Frame Selection, Frame All, Tidy Layout, Group into Compound, Ungroup Compound, Delete, Copy
  Graph, Paste Graph and Reload from Groom; **Presets** lists the presets (each **Apply** or
  **Insert After Selected Node**), **Save as Preset...**, **Copy Graph** and **Paste Graph**.
- **Ctrl+G** groups the selected nodes into one **Compound** node, **Ctrl+Shift+G** ungroups it so
  you can see and edit its nodes (Ctrl+Z groups them again), as in Maya. A compound is one node in
  Blender too, not a Blender node group: ungroup it to change what's inside, and pick which of its
  inner params show on it from **Show Inner Param**.
- Drop a node on a wire to insert it: GroomLab wires it in the way Maya does.
- **Tab** search lists your painted maps too: type a map's name (or "map") and pick it to add a
  Map node already reading it.
- **Backdrops** are Blender frames: **Add Backdrop Around Selection** (or Blender's **Ctrl+J**)
  frames nodes, and the frame's title, color (Blue, Green, Purple, Amber, Gray, as in Maya), place
  and size are saved with the graph, so Maya and the app show the same backdrop. Moving a backdrop
  moves the nodes in it.
- **Copy Graph** puts the whole graph on the clipboard as text (a preset with its control values);
  **Paste Graph** on another groom, in Blender, Maya or the app, fits it to that scalp.
- The **Node** tab of the editor's sidebar (**N**), the Node Settings, edits the selected node as
  Maya's param panel does: its name, type and what it does; its params by group (click a group's title to fold it,
  for every node of that type), **Advanced** folded; sliders over each param's usual range, which
  you can type past; lengths in scene units. **Ramps** are edited with Blender's curve widget. A
  node saved with an older behaviour offers **Upgrade**; a note (ⓘ) says when a node does nothing
  as set, errors show in red.
- Each param's **⌄** button opens its menu: **Set Key**, **Delete Key** and **Remove Animation**,
  **Expose as Input**, **Paint** (on the guides, the scalp's vertices or as a texture) and **Drive
  with Slider**. A keyed param reads a property of the groom's scalp object named `ctl_<node>_<param>`
  (a slider's is `ctl_<name>`): key it like any Blender property (**I** over it, or Set Key) and
  see its curve in Blender's Graph Editor and Dope Sheet; GroomLab reads it on every frame. Its
  values are in GroomLab's units (centimeters for lengths).
- The image button beside a mappable param feeds it from a **map**: a texture, a color set, an
  attribute, a painted map or an **expression**, over the range you give the map's black and
  white. An expression is edited in a text block (`GroomLab <node>.<param>.expr`, shown in an open
  Text Editor): it is checked as you type, the sidebar shows what's wrong, and the param takes it
  when it checks.
- **Script nodes**: **Add > New Script Node** adds one after the selected node and opens its script
  and header in two text blocks. Each change is checked (errors with their line in the sidebar) and
  taken by the node when it checks. **Save to Library** (or **Save as New**) shares it with every
  groom, Maya and the app.
- The **node library**: **Save as Node...** saves a compound to your node library
  (Documents/GroomLab/nodes, shared with Maya and the app), **Import Node...** copies a `.glnode`
  someone shared into it and adds it, **Open Nodes Folder** opens the folder. A library node whose
  file is newer than the copy in the groom shows **↑** on its corner: **Update Node** takes it.

Node positions are saved with the groom, where Maya's and the app's editors keep them: a groom laid
out in Blender opens laid out the same in Maya, and the other way round.

## Animation

GroomLab follows a deforming scalp, from an armature or shape keys: on each frame it reads the
mesh as Blender deforms it. With **Follow Scalp** on (the default) the groom is made on the scalp's
rest pose and each strand is carried along by its root, so the hair keeps its shape as the head
turns. Groom at a frame where the head is at rest.

Modifiers that change the mesh's vertex count (Subdivision Surface, Decimate...) can't be
followed: GroomLab then grows on the mesh before its modifiers.

## Rendering

Cycles and EEVEE render the hair object like any other Blender Curves object. A final render (F12,
or an animation) uses every strand: GroomLab switches the hair object to full density for the
render and back to the preview density afterwards, and turns on **Render > Lock Interface** for
that switch.

Each point's width (a diameter) is written as Blender's per-point `radius` (half the width), so
Cycles and EEVEE render the same coverage as Arnold to within about a percent. EEVEE needs
**Render > Curves > Shape** set to **Strip**; its default, Strand, draws every hair one pixel wide
regardless of width. GroomLab sets Strip when you create or open a groom, and the panel warns if it
has been set back to Strand.

The hair material reproduces GroomLab's Arnold hair shader with Blender's **Principled Hair BSDF**,
driven by the same HairColor, Bands, FeatherColor and RenderData nodes:

- **Pigment**: each strand's melanin and redness, with the node's dye over them.
- **RGB**: when the color comes from more than the pigment (a tip color, a map, hue, saturation
  or value randomness, a Bands or Feather Color node, a Blend of two looks), the strands' color,
  per point along them.
- **Both**, mixed strand by strand by its share, when the Hair Color node sets a Color Source; the
  pigment's look then varies in hue, saturation and value per strand as the RGB does.
- **Iridescence**: the structural color shifting with the viewing angle, mixed in where the
  Feather Color node puts it.
- **Highlight**: HairColor's specular and roughness (roughness per strand where it
  varies), and its opacity.

Arnold's and Cycles' hair models parameterize melanin differently, so a **GroomLab Hair Color**
node group converts melanin, redness, dye and color to the values that render in Cycles as they do
in Arnold (measured on the same grooms: average hair color within about 2-5%, comparable highlight
brightness).

The hair object carries these attributes, readable in your own shaders with an **Attribute** node.
Per-strand values use Blender's **Curve** domain, per-point values the **Point** domain:

| Attribute | Domain, type | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `radius` | Point, float | Half the strand width (Blender's own radius attribute) |
| `groomlab_strand_id` | Curve, 2 x int32 | The 64-bit strand ID, split into low and high 32 bits |
| `surface_uv_coordinate` | Curve, float2 | The scalp UV at the root (Blender's standard name) |
| `groomlab_color` | Curve, color | Linear RGB per strand |
| `groomlab_point_color` | Point, color | Linear RGB per point, when the color varies along the strand |
| `melanin`, `melaninRedness`, `specular`, `roughness`, `opacity`, `iridescence` | Curve or Point (as the graph wrote them), float | HairColor, RenderData and FeatherColor values, when the graph writes them |
| `groomlab_root_normal` | Curve, vector | The root normal used for iridescence (only with FeatherColor iridescence) |

Only the attributes in this table reach Blender. Other strand attributes (`clumpId`, Group masks,
Attribute node output) are not written to the hair object yet; export Alembic or USD to get them.
GroomLab does not modify a material you assign yourself.

What's approximate:

- **EEVEE** shades hair as a flat color without highlights or transmission. GroomLab gives it the
  color that matches Cycles' average, which is close for a full head of hair and slightly bright
  for light, sparse fur. Use Cycles to compare with Arnold.
- A HairColor **specular** below 0.2 renders a little shinier than in Arnold, and **iridescence**
  doesn't tint the highlight as Arnold's does.
- Compare colors with **Color Management > View Transform: Standard** (Blender's default, AgX,
  tones the image differently from Arnold's sRGB output).

### V-Ray

With **V-Ray for Blender** as the render engine, a final render (F12, or an animation) renders each
groom as GroomLab's V-Ray hair: the strands with each strand's color and a VRayHairNextMtl shader
set up from the groom's look, the same as GroomLab's V-Ray scene export
([Other renderers](/docs/other-renderers#v-ray)). V-Ray's own conversion of the hair object would
lose the colors and the look. Nothing needs setting up:

- Before the render, GroomLab writes the groom (every strand) as a V-Ray scene in your temp folder
  (`groomlab_vray`) and renders it through a **V-Ray Scene** object it adds under the scalp
  (`<scalp>_vray`, hidden in the viewport). For that render the hair object is hidden; afterwards
  both are as they were, so Cycles and EEVEE render as before.
- The hair follows the scalp: move, rotate or scale it as usual. (The scalp's scale goes into the
  strands themselves, since V-Ray does not scale hair widths with an object's transform; a scalp
  scaled unevenly gets the average of its scale.)
- **Animation:** with V-Ray's animation mode on, the scene holds the whole frame range as V-Ray
  keys. **Motion blur:** with V-Ray's motion blur on, it holds a frame more on each side, and V-Ray
  blurs between them.
- A scalp that deforms (an armature, shape keys), rendered from Blender's window, keeps the pose of
  the frame the render starts on for the whole animation and its motion blur.
- **Interactive (viewport) rendering** in V-Ray shows V-Ray's own conversion of the hair object, as
  a preview without GroomLab's colors and look. Use a final render to judge the hair.
- The file is temporary and written on the machine that renders: on a render farm, render the
  `.blend` with GroomLab and V-Ray for Blender installed on each machine.


## Files

- **Save Groom File As...** writes the groom in GroomLab's file conventions (Y up, centimeters).
  Maya and the GroomLab app open it as evaluated in Blender, with the same guides.
- **Import Groom File...** opens a groom from Maya, the app or Blender on the selected mesh, or with
  the scalp stored in the file (**Use the File's Scalp**) when you don't have the head in Blender.
- **Import > Alembic Curves...** takes the selected groom's guides from an Alembic file's curves (an
  **ImportCurves** node rooted on the scalp; with a mesh selected and no groom, a groom is made on
  it first). The curves are read as GroomLab's and Maya's Alembic files have them: Y up,
  centimeters. **Utilities > Bake Node to Guides...** makes them guides you can brush.
- The groom remembers the file it was opened from or saved to: **Save to Groom File** writes it
  again, and **Reload from Groom File** reads it back (after editing it in the GroomLab app),
  keeping the mesh.
- A **.blend** file stores the whole groom (guides, groups, maps, the selection), so reopening it
  restores the hair and the Groom Tools state.

## The GroomLab menu

The top bar's **GroomLab** menu is Maya's GroomLab menu, in its order, with what Blender runs:
**Groom Tools**, **Graph Editor**, **Create Groom from Selection**, **Add Sample Mesh**, **Guides
from Photos (experimental)...**, **Groom Files** (Import Groom File..., Reload from Groom File, Save
to Groom File, Save Groom File As...), **Import** (Alembic Curves...), **Add**, **Simulate**, **Review**, **Export**, **Utilities** and **Help** (Docs, Keyboard Shortcuts,
Check for Updates... (the newest version, installed in place), Discord,
Support GroomLab, About GroomLab). Items Blender doesn't run yet are left out. Blender's **File >
Import** and **File > Export** have **GroomLab Groom File (.groom)** too.

The Groom Tools' **Output** section has **Simulate Range**, the **Export** and **Import** menus and
**Transfer...**.

## Add

**Add** puts the graph's building blocks in for you, as Maya's Add menu does:

- **Feathers...**: a feather design (contour, down, flight, afterfeather, barred, or a
  `feathers_<name>` preset of yours), or several separated by commas with their shares in
  **Weights**. **Scattered feathers (shaped by the guides)** scatters them over the scalp, laid the
  way the guides are combed; **One feather per guide (hand-placed)** makes each guide of the
  **Guide Group** (`feathers`, another of the groom's groups, or a new name such as `flight_left`
  or `tail`) one feather, and the selected curve objects (drawn from the scalp, the first point the
  root) join that group. A group with no guides yet gets the **Draw** tool drawing into it. The
  feathers merge with the hair before the output ([Hand-placed flight
  feathers](/docs/fur-and-feathers#hand-placed-flight-feathers)).
- **Braid on Curves...**: the selected curve objects become the **braids** guide group (without
  any, the group's guides are the paths: draw them with **Draw**), and a **Braid** node after the
  Interpolate braids the strands along them, in the style you pick (three, fishtail, rope,
  cornrow).
- **Cards > Hero / Mid / Low**: a **Cards** node before the output (one is made when the graph has
  none), set for that level of detail: hero 6 x 2 cards a clump with flyaways, mid 2 x 1, low one
  flat card a clump.
- **Collider**: each selected mesh (besides the groom) keeps the strands off it: a **ColliderMesh**
  and a **Collide** node before the output. The mesh follows its animation and edits: GroomLab
  sends it again whenever it moves or changes.
- **Wind**, **Jiggle**: a node before the output.

## Simulate

GroomLab's own hair simulation runs in Blender as in Maya and the app:

- **Add Simulate Node** puts a **Simulate** node between the guides and the Interpolate: play the
  timeline to see it.
- **Simulate Range** steps the Simulate nodes over the playback range (the preview range when it
  is on) into their cache, so scrubbing reads it. The status bar counts the frames; **Esc** stops,
  keeping the frames made. The scalp's animation over the range (armature, shape keys) is handed
  to the simulation first.
- **Bake Simulation** simulates the range and writes each Simulate node's frames as an Alembic
  guide cache in the `.blend`'s `groomlab/caches` folder (the temp folder for an unsaved file):
  renders, exports and groom files read the bake.
- **Clear Simulation** drops the frames and the bakes: the next frame shown simulates again.

## Transfer Groom to Scalp

**Utilities > Transfer Groom to Scalp...** (or **Transfer...** in the Output section) moves the
groom onto another mesh: a new sculpt, a retopologized head, another character. Pick the **New
Scalp** (the selected mesh by default) and the **Method**:

- **Closest point**: where the new scalp is nearest (the same place and size).
- **Fit bounding boxes**: the old scalp's bounds moved and scaled onto the new one's first.
- **Markers: align** and **Markers: warp**: the marker pairs stored in the groom (picked in Maya
  or the GroomLab app) give the move, turn and scale, or bend space onto other proportions.
- **Matching UVs**: both meshes laid out alike in UV; each root at its UV.

**Keep guide lengths**, **Fit groom to the new scalp's size** and **Project painted maps** work as
in Maya. With **The new scalp owns the groom** (on by default) the new mesh is the groom from then
on: its hair and guides objects, graph, sliders and correctives move to it, and the old mesh is a
plain mesh again. The two meshes are compared in their own object space, so a new head placed
elsewhere in the scene takes the groom as it sits on the old one. **Ctrl+Z** in Blender, or
GroomLab's own **Undo**, puts the groom back on the old mesh.

## Review and utilities

- **Review > Density Heatmap** colors the strands by root density; **Strand Count HUD** shows the
  active groom's strand count (and at render), guide count and evaluation time in the viewport's
  corner; **Turntable** makes a camera orbiting the groom over 120 frames with a key, fill and rim
  light, and the viewport looks through it.
- **Utilities > Strands to Mesh...** makes the strands one mesh object under the scalp: tubes (of
  the sides you pick) or ribbons along the scalp, UVs root to tip, at most the strands you set
  (thinned by strand ID).
- **Utilities > Bake Node to Guides...** turns a graph node's strands or guides (the groom's output,
  a Scatter, a ToGuides...) as they are now into guides you can brush, in a root group (**baked**
  by default: made when new, added to otherwise), at the density you pick. The Graph Editor's node
  menu has it too.
- **Utilities > Bake Scale Maps** bakes a scales groom into its skin's UVs as normal, height and
  color maps (PNG, UDIM tiles too), in the `.blend`'s `groomlab/bakes` folder.
- **Utilities > Copy Graph to Other Grooms...** gives the other selected grooms the active groom's
  graph and slider values; their guides stay. Painted maps the graph reads that a groom lacks are
  listed: paint or import them there.
- **Utilities > Find Missing Files...** relinks the selected grooms' (else every groom's) missing
  texture maps, image and Alembic files and linked groom files to files of the same name in a
  folder you pick and its subfolders. When nothing is missing it says so, without asking for a
  folder.

## Export

**Alembic** and **USD** export the strands (the current frame or the scene's range, every strand
by default) in GroomLab's space: Y up, centimeters for Alembic, `metersPerUnit = 0.01` for USD.
Blender's importers convert both. The files carry the same attributes as Maya's exports
([Strand attributes](/docs/rendering#strand-attributes)).

The engine exports write what Maya's write ([Game export](/docs/game-export)), at the current frame:

- **Unreal Groom...**: Alembic in Unreal's groom format, strands plus the primary guides.
- **Unreal Cards...**: the cards as FBX with TGA maps in Unreal's hair card layout and a manifest,
  into a folder.
- **Unity Strands (.abc)...**: Alembic curves for Unity's hair package.
- **Unity Feathers...**: the barbs as strand groups, the rachis FBX and the Hair Asset manifest,
  into a folder.
- **Unity Cards...**: the cards as FBX (meters, Y up) with PNG maps and a manifest, into a folder.
- **glTF Cards...**: a `.glb` with the maps inside, or a `.gltf` beside its files.
- **Scales Mesh (FBX)...** and **Scales Instancer (USD)...**: a scales groom's scales bent onto the
  skin as one mesh, or a USD PointInstancer of their plates.

The FBX files are written with Blender's own FBX exporter from the meshes GroomLab makes.

**Card Maps...** bakes the groom's cards (add a **Cards** node to the graph first) and writes
their texture maps as a preset packs them: pick the preset (the ones Maya and the app list,
your own included), the resolution, format, bit depth, padding and supersampling
([Export card maps](/docs/game-export#export-card-maps)). The packing table is in Maya and the app.

## Not in Blender yet

- **Jiggle** nodes: the panel marks them. Jiggle passes the strands through in Blender (it isn't
  fed the scalp's earlier frames yet), so don't rely on it in a Blender render yet.
- The tube **outline editor**, and the **Guides from Photos window** (flow arrows and mask touch-ups
  drawn on the photo, the measured look): use Maya or the GroomLab app. Picking transfer **marker**
  points is Maya's and the app's too; Blender's transfer uses the markers stored in the groom.
- The Graph Editor's **GPU preview** (Maya's viewport).
- The **XGen** and **Yeti** importers (Maya): import there, save a `.groom` file and open it in
  Blender.
- Grooms made with earlier previews keep their guides as a Blender Curves object you sculpt with
  Blender's Curves Sculpt brushes (**Sculpt Guides**); make a new groom for the Groom Tools.
- macOS and Linux builds.
- Keep the scalp object's scale at 1 (apply it): presets fit the groom to the mesh's own size.

## What's open source

All of it. The add-on (its Python code) is free software under GPL-3.0-or-later. Its source is
included in the extension. GroomLab's engine runs in `groomlab-helper`, a separate program the
add-on starts and talks to. It is never loaded into Blender. The helper is open source too, under
the Apache License 2.0 (`bin/LICENSE-HELPER.txt` inside the extension), and its source is in
[GroomLab's repository](https://github.com/thekesslereffect/groomlab).
