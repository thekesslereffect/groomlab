## Fur

Fur uses the same graph as hair: Scatter, Interpolate from guides, then Clump, Noise and other
look nodes. Short fur can be groomed without guides:

1. Draw direction arrows on the body with the **Flow** brush ([Guides & brushes](/docs/guides-and-brushes#short-hair-without-guides)).
2. Load the `creature_fur` preset, or build `Scatter → Length → Flow → Width`; the **Flow** node
   orients the strands along the arrows.
3. Add guides only where you need tufts and cowlicks: the `flow_with_guides` preset blends the
   flow fur with the guided hair by a painted map, `guideAreas`, made black (all fur) when you apply
   it. Paint it white in Groom Tools > Paint where the guides should take over. (Or give
   Interpolate a `limitDistance` so the guides only affect strands near them:
   [Guide influence](/docs/node-graph#interpolate-and-guide-influence).)

- **Fur only near the guides:** Scatter's `guideRadius` keeps only roots within that distance of a
  guide, for a patch, a stripe or a mane.
- **Fur parting around whiskers, horns or eyes:** give a **Clump** a negative `strength` (down to
  -1), connect the whiskers' guides to its `centers` input and set `centersFrom` to
  `inputCurves`. Fur within `repelRadius` of a whisker is pushed out to that distance along the
  skin, keeping its length. Grow the whiskers themselves from those guides with ToStrands and
  Merge them with the fur.
- **Banded, tipped and shaded coats** (`agouti_fur`): see [Color patterns](/docs/color-patterns).

## Feathers

Feathers are generated procedurally. Every strand fed to a **Propagate** node (Add Node >
Feathers) is treated as a **shaft** and grows barbs, and the barbs are ordinary strands for the
nodes after it: Noise, Curl, Width, HairColor, FeatherColor, Clump and Cards all apply to them.

<Example name="feathers.png" alt="Feathers grown with GroomLab" caption="Feathers" />

### Add feathers

**GroomLab > Add > Feathers...** asks for a design and how the feathers grow:

- **Scattered feathers (shaped by the guides):** feathers scattered over the skin and laid the way
  your guides are combed, so combing the guides along the body lays the feathers with them. For a
  body of contour or down feathers. Python: `groomlab.add_feathers(groom, "contour")`.
- **One feather per guide (hand-placed):** each guide of a guide group is exactly one feather. For
  flight feathers and tails, where every feather is placed by hand. See
  [Hand-placed flight feathers](#hand-placed-flight-feathers).
- **Assortments:** several designs separated by commas, with their shares
  (`groomlab.add_feathers(groom, ["contour", "down"], weights=[3, 1])`): a **Split** node (outputs
  `a` to `d`, by fraction or by a painted map) gives each design its share of the feathers.

### Hand-placed flight feathers

For wings and tails, place every feather yourself: each guide you draw is one feather.

1. Start from the **Flight feathers (hand-placed)** preset (Presets > Feathers), or on an existing
   groom pick **Add > Feathers...** with **One feather per guide (hand-placed)**.
2. Pick the **Guide group** the feathers go into: `feathers` by default, or type a new name to keep
   parts apart (`flight_left`, `flight_right`, `tail`). Run Add > Feathers once per group.
3. Draw guides from the skin with the **Draw** tool (the preset and Add > Feathers pick it for you,
   drawing into the group), or select curves drawn from the skin before Add > Feathers: each curve
   joins the group.
4. Each guide is one feather. Comb, brush or delete guides to place, shape or remove feathers.

The feather groups are left out of the guides that grow the hair, so hand-placed feathers never
change the hair or body feathers around them. When the groom already has a feathers group, Add >
Feathers starts on One feather per guide with that group picked.

Python, one group per part:

```python
groomlab.add_feathers(groom, "flight", source="guides", curves=left, group="flight_left")
groomlab.add_feathers(groom, "flight", source="guides", curves=tail, group="tail")
groomlab.feather_guide_groups(groom)  # ["flight_left", "tail"]
```

### Designs

| Design | Look |
| --- | --- |
| `contour` | Rounded vane lying on the body, with a few splits |
| `down` | Short shafts, fluffy curly barbs all around |
| `flight` | Long, stiff, asymmetric vane |
| `afterfeather` | A contour vane whose base turns to fluff, like a chick's |
| `barred` | White bars across one vane, the other iridescent green, a white downy base ([Color patterns](/docs/color-patterns)) |

Each design is a **Compound**: right-click it > **Ungroup Compound** (Ctrl+Shift+G) to see and
edit its nodes. On Propagate, `lengthProfile` defines the vane outline, `angle` the barb angle to
the shaft, `curl` bends the barbs, `droop` cups the vane, and `splits` gathers barbs into the gaps
real feathers have.

- **Downy base:** Propagate's `down` (0.2-0.4) turns the barbs at the base into soft, curly down
  (`downCount`, `downLength`, `downCurl`, `downFrizz`), colored by FeatherColor's `downColor`.
- **Fluffier base:** a second Propagate on the same shafts (`start` 0, `end` about 0.4, longer
  barbs with `droop` and `lift`), followed by Curl and Noise. A third Propagate on those barbs
  grows barbules. Give each branch its own HairColor and join them with Merge; the
  `afterfeather` design is built this way.
- **Blending vane into fluff:** the per-strand `shaftU` attribute gives a barb's position on its
  shaft (0 root, 1 tip). An expression such as `1 - smoothstep(0.2, 0.5, $shaftU)` is 1 at the
  base and 0 further up the vane; use it for Noise `amplitude` and Curl `radius` to break up the
  lowest barbs into fluff. Multiply by `1 - $isShaft` to exclude the shaft itself.

Propagate writes these per-strand attributes, available to expressions, maps and renderers:
`featherId`, `shaftU`, `side`, `barbIndex`, `isShaft`, `isDown` (with down), `vaneU`,
`vaneTipU`, `vaneTipV`, `vaneNormalX/Y/Z`, `clumpId` and `groupId`
([Strand attributes](/docs/rendering#strand-attributes)).

### Layered feathers with Plume

Each feather is grown independently, so neighboring vanes intersect and drooping barbs go under
the skin. A **Plume** node after the feathers (similar to Yeti's Plume) stacks them: it sorts the
feathers and rotates each one up about its root until it lies `offset` above the feathers under
it and `bodyOffset` above the body (the scalp, and any mesh on its `collider` input). The
`layered_feathers` preset adds one after the contour design.

| Param | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `rankBy` | Which feathers lie on top: `rootDistance` (rooted farther along `direction`; with `direction` (0, 0, 0) each tip lies over the base of the one behind it, like roof tiles), `length` (shorter over longer: coverts over flight feathers), `channel` (`rankChannel`) or `random`. `invert` reverses the order. |
| `stiffness` | 1 rotates each feather rigidly; lower values bend it instead. |
| `maxAngle` | Maximum rotation of a feather off the body, in degrees. |
| `strength` | Fraction of its rotation each feather takes (mappable, per feather). |

The base of each feather stays rooted, and feathers facing different directions (the two sides
of a wing) are not stacked on each other. A few thousand feathers take well under a second.

### Meshes along strands

An **Instance** node places a mesh on the strands. With `deform` `alongStrand` each copy is bent
along its strand (similar to Yeti's instance deformation): the mesh's long axis (`deformAxis`, +Y
by default; model it from its base at the origin) is mapped along the strand from `tMin` to
`tMax`. Use it for a modeled feather following its shaft (`tMin` and `tMax` 0, `sizeMode`
`fitStrand`), leaves on vines (the `vine_leaves` preset) or ribbons. Set `spinRandom` to 0 to keep
each copy aligned with its vane. Bent copies render and export as one mesh. See
[Rendering](/docs/rendering#instances).

### The rachis

`shaftWidth` turns each shaft into a tube mesh that can be skinned with the character in a game
engine, with the barbs rooted on its surface. **Shaft sides** sets the sides around it and **Shaft
segments** the segments along it: 0 puts a ring at every point of the shaft, a number lays that
many evenly along a smooth curve through them, so a faceted rachis turns round without a Resample
node (the barbs and the shafts stay as they are). See [Game export](/docs/game-export#feathers-in-engines).

## Scales

Scales are rigid plates positioned by strands. The **Scales** node (Look) places one plate at
each strand's root, lying on the skin along the strand: its length is the strand length times
`size`, its width the strand's root width. Everything that shapes strands therefore shapes the
scales: Transform's `scale` and Width size them, Bend, Flow and the guides orient them, HairColor
colors them.

1. Load `dragon_scales`, or `scales_from_guides` or `scales_from_flow` to orient them with your
   guides or flow arrows.
2. Comb the guides (or draw flow arrows) and the scales rotate with them.
3. Paint with the **Density** brush to thin them out.

| Param | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `prototype` | The plate: the built-in `groomlab:scale` (keeled), `groomlab:scaleRound` and `groomlab:scaleShield`, or your own mesh (1 unit long along +Y, top facing +Z). Several names mix them, at random or by a map (`variantBy`, `variant`). |
| `tile`, `lift` | `tile` lifts each tip over the base of the next scale, like roof tiles; `lift` tilts them further. |
| `conform`, `offset` | Bend each plate over the body's curvature without going inside it; raise the plates off the skin. |
| `follow` | `strand` bends each plate along its strand's curve: swept scales, spikes and quills (the `swept_spikes` preset). |
| `aim` | Orientation on the skin: toward the strand's tip (`tip`, the default) or its direction near the root (`root`), for strands that curl back. |
| `preview` | `quads` (or `auto` on very large sets) draws flat quads while grooming; renders always use the plates. |

For shading and exporting scales, see [Rendering](/docs/rendering#scales).
