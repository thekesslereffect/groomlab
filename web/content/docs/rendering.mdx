## Render in Arnold

1. Render as usual. A groom without a shader of its own renders with a hair shader made from its
   colors, as the viewport shows them.
2. To tune the shader in Hypershade, click **Create Hair Shader** on the HairColor node (or run
   `groomlab.create_hair_shader(groom)`).

MtoA loads GroomLab's translator automatically. The translator evaluates the graph at full density
(regardless of `previewDensity`) and exports native Arnold `curves` (linear basis, one curve per
strand, `radius` = half the strand width), with deformation motion blur when motion blur is on.
The curves carry the strand attributes as Arnold user data ([Strand attributes](#strand-attributes)).

## Maya Hardware 2.0

**GroomLab > Render > Maya Hardware 2.0 (no renderer needed)...** renders with Maya's own Hardware 2.0 renderer: nothing to install or license, in every Maya
version. It takes well under a second to a few seconds a frame, so use it for quick lookdev, turntables
and previews while you groom; use Arnold (or [another renderer](/docs/other-renderers)) for final
pictures.

Choose the frames (the current frame, the playback range or the render range), the size and the
quality (Draft, Preview or Final: 4, 8 or 16 anti-aliasing samples a pixel), then **Render**. The
frames render one after another in this Maya, with a progress window; **Stop** ends the render after
the frame it is on. The images go to the scene's `groomlab/<scene>/renders/hw2_<date>` folder, and the
last one opens in the Render View. From Python: `groomlab.render_hardware2(start=1, end=120)`.

What the render shows:

- **The scene as the viewport draws it**, through the studio's camera when there is a studio, else
  the view's camera.
- **The groom at its render density.** Every groom draws as a render shows it: `renderDensity` (not
  `previewDensity`) with no point budget, the render width and length scales, and nodes set to render
  only. Guides, flow and painted maps are hidden, and the hair draws with GroomLab's hair shading,
  width as coverage and its self-shadowing.
- **The studio's lighting.** Hardware 2.0 cannot use Arnold's area lights, so each one is replaced by
  a Maya light for the render, about as bright as Arnold's on what it faces, light links kept. The
  sky dome lights as it is. The backdrop and the mannequin render in their colors, as Arnold shows
  them. Without a studio, the scene's Maya lights light it, or Maya's default light when there are none.
- **The background.** The studio's backdrop, or else transparency: the PNG's alpha is 0 where the
  camera sees nothing.
- **Images as the viewport shows them**, with Maya's color management view (sRGB, ACES).

Everything the render changes (render settings, the groom's display, the stand-in lights and
materials) is put back after the last frame, outside the undo queue.

Hardware 2.0 shows what the viewport can, not what Arnold computes: no light bounces, so shadowed
sides are darker; hard shadows from the meshes instead of soft area-light shadows; and the viewport's
hair shading, close to Arnold's look but not the same (fine hair lying on the skin shows less than in
Arnold).

## Studio lighting

**GroomLab > Studio** puts a photo studio round
the active groom in one click, for showcase renders in Arnold. Pick a preset, then render as usual:
the viewport looks through the studio's camera.

| Preset | Look |
| --- | --- |
| **Portrait** | A large softbox key from the upper left, a soft fill from the right, a cool rim from behind on the right and a kicker on the left to lift the hair off the backdrop, a soft glow on the mid grey wall behind the head. |
| **Beauty** | Clamshell: the key above the camera, a fill from below, two soft rims. Even and flattering. |
| **Rim Drama** | A near-black backdrop, a low fill and two strong rims from behind: the silhouette and every flyaway show. |
| **High Key** | A bright backdrop and soft, even light from the front, with few shadows. |
| **Turntable** | The portrait lighting with the groom turning round once over 120 frames (the playback and render range are set). |

Everything goes under one group, `groomlab_studio`:

- a seamless **cyclorama**: a floor under the groom curving up into a back wall, in a slightly rough
  grey `aiStandardSurface` (`groomlab_studio_backdropMtl`);
- Arnold **area lights** (`aiAreaLight`: key, fill, rims, kicker, a background light) and a dim
  neutral **sky dome** (no HDR file needed; it fills the shadows but is not seen by the camera);
- the camera **`groomlab_studio_cam`**: an 85 mm portrait lens, three-quarter on, the head centered
  with headroom (long hair or a body below is cropped to the head and shoulders). It becomes the only
  renderable camera;
- a matte light grey **mannequin** material (`groomlab_studio_mannequinMtl`) on the groom's scalp,
  its other scalps and its colliders, so the hair reads against a neutral head;
- **render settings** for hair: camera (AA) samples 5, specular and transmission samples 2,
  specular depth 2, transmission depth 10, and 1920 x 1080. Color management is left as it is.

The studio is sized and placed from the groom's bounds and turned for the scene's up axis (Y or Z),
so a head in centimeters, in meters or ten times too big gets the same picture. Picking another preset
relights the same rig in place. Building it is one undo step.

**Studio > Remove Studio** deletes the rig and its materials and puts back what it changed: the head
meshes' own materials, the render settings, the cameras' renderable flags and the playback range.
**Studio > Look Through Studio Camera** looks through the camera again after you leave it.

```python
groomlab.add_studio(groom, preset="rim_drama")   # groomlab.studio.preset_names() lists them
groomlab.add_studio(groom, mannequin=False, render_settings=False, resolution=None)
groomlab.remove_studio()
```

Presets are data: add one to `groomlab.studio.PRESETS` (backdrop grey, sky dome level, camera angle
and lens, and per light its angle round and above the groom from the camera, distance and size in
groom sizes, exposure, color and spread).

Tips:

- The lights are tuned for Maya's default color management (ACEScg with the ACES view). Adjust the
  lights' **Exposure** to taste; they keep their brightness at any scale.
- Rim Drama shows stray hairs and flyaways best; High Key shows the color and the density.
- To keep the head's own materials, pass `mannequin=False` (or reassign them after; Remove Studio
  puts back what it found).
- **GroomLab > Render > RenderMan (prman)...** and **V-Ray Standalone...** render the studio too
  (its camera, its area lights and dome as their lights, the backdrop and the head in their greys),
  from any Maya version ([RenderMan](/docs/other-renderers#render-in-renderman-any-maya-tested),
  [V-Ray](/docs/other-renderers#v-ray)).
- The app's **Look** mode has lighting presets of its own (studio, daylight, overcast, sunset, rim)
  ([Look](/docs/app#look)).

## Viewport shading

Maya's viewport (DirectX 11 and OpenGL) shades the strands with GroomLab's own hair shading
(`displayHairShading` **GroomLab**, the default): a three-lobe hair model similar to Arnold's
`aiStandardHair`, using the strands' color and HairColor's `specular` and `roughness`, lit by
the viewport lights. The standalone app uses the same shading. `displayHairShading` **Maya**
switches back to Maya's own hair shading.

- **Self-shadowing:** the hair shadows itself, with forward scattering through the volume
  (`displaySelfShadowing`); light-colored hair also gets multiple scattering, so blond hair stays
  bright when backlit.
- **Variation:** each strand's shading varies slightly from its neighbors (`displayStrandVariation`).
- **Cards** are shaded like the strands they replace.
- **Width as coverage:** a strand thinner than a pixel is drawn with partial coverage
  (`displayWidthCoverage`), so tapered tips fade and dense hair seen from a distance looks as dense
  as in the render. `displayCoverageDensity` compensates a reduced preview density so it reads as
  dense as the full groom.
- **Clump level of detail:** at a distance, each clump draws only as many strands as cover its
  pixels (`displayClumpLod`). A large groom covering a few hundred pixels draws several times
  faster.
- All of this works with Maya's multisample anti-aliasing on or off.

Renders and exports always use every strand, at full density.

## Hair shader

**Create Hair Shader** on the HairColor node (or `groomlab.create_hair_shader(groom)`) creates an
`aiStandardHair` that reads the per-strand color written by HairColor, and assigns it. To use your
own shading group, run `groomlab.assign_shading_group(groom, sg)`.

- **Color:** the shader's base color reads the RGB user data `hair_color` (per strand, or per
  point with a tip color, Bands or FeatherColor), with `melanin` at 0. That RGB is the whole hair
  color: in HairColor's `melanin` mode it is the color whose absorption is the melanin's (so it
  renders as `aiStandardHair`'s melanin would), dyed by the dye, with every strand's randomness in
  it. The viewport turns the same RGB into the fiber's absorption with Chiang et al. 2016's mapping.
  Arnold absorbs a base color about 1.18 times more than that mapping, so the shader passes the RGB
  through an `aiColorCorrect` with gamma 1.084 first; the render then matches the viewport, and a
  melanin's color renders within about 5% of `aiStandardHair`'s own melanin. A groom without a color node
  renders by its `melanin` and `melaninRedness` attributes, if any. The shader follows later
  changes of the look (the Graph Editor and the Attribute Editor's Hair Color section make it
  again when the look needs another network); a shader of your own is never changed.
- **Specular and roughness:** HairColor's **Highlight** group sets `specular` (strength, default
  0.3), `roughness` (default 0.5) and `tilt` (cuticle tilt in degrees, default 3), each mappable
  and with a per-strand random spread. The viewport and Create Hair Shader both use them.
- **Opacity:** a **RenderData** node sets `opacity` times `opacityProfile` (root to tip), with
  `opacityRandom` per strand. When any point is below 1, the curves are exported with
  `opaque` off so Arnold evaluates the shader's opacity.
- **Iridescence** (FeatherColor): Create Hair Shader adds a small network that mixes in the
  iridescent color by the angle between the camera and each strand's root normal, close to the
  viewport result.
- **Your own shader:** read any strand attribute with an `aiUserDataFloat` (or `aiUserDataColor`
  for `hair_color` and `root_normal`) whose **Attribute** is the attribute name. See
  [Strand attributes](#strand-attributes).

The same RGB reaches Unreal, USD, Alembic, RenderMan and the cards.

### Color values

Every color param (HairColor's `color`, `dye` and `tipColor`, Bands' and FeatherColor's colors)
holds linear values in the rendering space, as Maya's color attributes and Arnold's colors do. The
values reach Arnold and the exports unchanged. The swatches in the Graph Editor and the Attribute
Editor show a color as Maya shows it: through the view transform when color management is on, as
it is when it is off. The standalone app treats the values as linear sRGB and shows them with the
sRGB transfer curve.

### Base color and appearance

A hair color has two forms. The **base color** is what a hair shader takes: `aiStandardHair`'s base
color, RenderMan's `rman_color`, the viewport's and the app's hair shading, Blender's Cycles hair.
It is dark for natural hair: medium brown (melanin 0.35) is about (0.04, 0.015, 0.003), because the
shader darkens it further as light passes through many strands. The **appearance** is what the
hair looks like: the matte color it matches under even white light, measured with Arnold (a lock
of hair a few strands deep, the highlight included). Medium brown looks about (0.28, 0.15, 0.05),
sRGB (145, 108, 66); white stays white. Renderers that run a hair shader get the base color; what
stores or draws a plain color gets the appearance:

| Gets the base color | Gets the appearance |
| --- | --- |
| Arnold `hair_color` (translator, procedural, Create Hair Shader) | USD `primvars:displayColor` |
| RenderMan `hair_color`, `rman_color` | Alembic `Cd` |
| Maya viewport with GroomLab's hair shading | Unreal `groom_color` |
| The app's lookdev | The cards' Color maps (Unreal, Unity, glTF) and card tints |
| Blender's Cycles material | Maya's hair tube shading (`displayHairShading` Maya), the app's plain modes |

The graph's `colorR`, `colorG`, `colorB` attributes hold the base color. Maya's hair shading
draws baked cards by turning their appearance back into a base color. Scales take HairColor's RGB
as their surface color, as it is.

## Strand attributes

Every strand attribute in GroomLab is a named float array, either **per strand** (one value per
curve: uniform) or **per point** (one value per vertex: varying/vertex). Integers such as
`clumpId` are stored as floats, and colors as three float attributes (`colorR`, `colorG`,
`colorB`). Nodes write attributes into the graph ([Attributes](/docs/node-graph#attributes)), and
the renderers and exporters pass them on **under the same name**, without a prefix. The only
renaming is in USD (see below).

### Built-in data

Besides the attributes the graph writes, every output carries the strands' geometry, an ID, the
root UV and a color:

| Data | Arnold curves | RenderMan curves | Alembic `.abc` | USD `.usda` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Points | `points`, one key per motion key | `vertex point P` | `P` | `points` |
| Width | `radius` (per point, half the width) | `vertex float width` (diameter) | `width` (vertex scope, diameter) | `widths` (`vertex`, diameter) |
| Strand ID | `strand_id`, `uniform UINT` (low 32 bits) | `strand_id`, `uniform int` (low 32 bits); `hair_index`, `uniform float` | `strandId`, uint64, uniform scope | `primvars:strandId`, `int64[]`, `uniform` |
| Root UV (with scalp UVs) | the curves' own `uvs`, one per curve | `st` and `scalp_st`, `uniform float[2]` | the curves' `uvs`, uniform scope | `primvars:st`, `texCoord2f[]`, `uniform` |
| Color (linear RGB) | `hair_color` (the base color), `uniform RGB`, or `varying RGB` when the color varies along the strand | `hair_color` (the base color), `uniform color` or `vertex color` | `Cd` (the appearance), C3f, uniform scope (per strand only) | `primvars:displayColor` (the appearance), `color3f[]`, `uniform` (per strand only) |

- The **strand ID** is a stable 64-bit integer per strand: the same strand keeps it on every frame
  and at any density, which is what motion blur and caches match strands by. Arnold and RenderMan
  receive its low 32 bits.
- The **root UV** is the scalp's UV at the strand's root, constant along the strand. It is only
  written when the scalp has UVs. In Alembic, the `vertexUVs` flag of
  `groomLabExportAlembic` writes it once per point instead of once per curve.
- The **color** is HairColor's RGB (after any Bands or FeatherColor), or the color of its melanin
  when there is no RGB, as a base color for renderers and as the appearance for Alembic and USD
  ([Base color and appearance](#base-color-and-appearance)). It is written whenever the graph has
  a color node.
- No parametric coordinate along the strand is written. For a root-to-tip value in a shader, use
  the renderer's own curve parameterization.

### Attributes written by nodes

| Attribute | Per | Written by | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `melanin` | strand | HairColor | Each strand's melanin after its randomness and greys, 0 (white, blond) to 1 (black), as data (renders by `hair_color`); 0 in `color` mode |
| `melaninRedness` | strand | HairColor | 0 (brown) to 1 (red), as data |
| `colorR`, `colorG`, `colorB` | strand | HairColor, Bands, FeatherColor | The strand's linear RGB (also in the built-in color) |
| `colorR`, `colorG`, `colorB` | point | A HairColor tip color, Bands, FeatherColor | The RGB along the strand |
| `specular`, `roughness`, `tilt` | strand | HairColor, only when set away from their defaults (0.3, 0.5, 3) | Highlight strength, highlight spread, cuticle tilt in degrees |
| `opacity` | point | RenderData | 0-1, root to tip |
| `wetness` | point | Wet, Simulate with water or rain | 0 dry to 1 soaked |
| `iridescence` | point | FeatherColor | Amount of iridescent color |
| `clumpId` | strand | Clump (the name is its `clumpChannel`), Wet, Propagate | The strand's clump index, -1 for none |
| `region` | strand | Interpolate with a `regionMap` (name: `regionChannel`) | Region index, -1 for none |
| `guideInfluence` | strand | Interpolate with `limitDistance` above 0 (name: `influenceChannel`) | Guide influence, 0-1 |
| `tubeId`, `tubeU`, `tubeV` | strand | Interpolate with `fillTubes` | The tube the strand fills (-1 for none) and its place in the tube's cross-section, -1 to 1 ([Tube guides](/docs/guides-and-brushes#tube-guides)) |
| Guide attribute names | strand, or point for layers painted along guides | Interpolate | Attributes and painted layers on the guides, interpolated to the strands |
| Group name | strand | Group | Mask, 1 inside, 0 outside |
| Attribute node `name` | strand | Attribute | Any value: a constant, a map, an expression |
| `braidId`, `braidFlat` | strand | Braid | Braid index and flattening |
| `flyaway` | strand | Flyaway | Which strands are flyaways |
| `blend` | strand | Blend (name: `mixChannel`) | The mix applied to each strand |
| `featherId`, `shaftU`, `side`, `barbIndex`, `isShaft`, `isDown`, `vaneU`, `vaneTipU`, `vaneTipV`, `vaneNormalX/Y/Z`, `groupId` | strand | Propagate | Feather structure ([Fur & feathers](/docs/fur-and-feathers#feathers)) |

Attributes whose names start with `_` are internal and removed at the Output node.

### What each output carries

| | Per-strand attributes | Per-point attributes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Arnold** (translator and `groomlab_procedural`) | Every one, as user data `uniform FLOAT` | Only `opacity`, `iridescence` and `wetness` (`varying FLOAT`) and the per-point color (in `hair_color`) |
| **RenderMan** (`groomlab_renderman`) | Every one, as `uniform float` primvars | Only `opacity`, `iridescence` and `wetness` (`vertex float`) and the per-point color (in `hair_color`) |
| **Alembic** | Every one, as a float arbGeomParam, uniform scope | None (USD carries them) |
| **USD** | Every one, as `primvars:<name>`, `float[]`, `uniform` | Every one, as `primvars:<name>`, `float[]`, `vertex` |
| **Blender** hair object | A fixed set used by the hair material ([Blender](/docs/blender#rendering)) | The point color, `iridescence`, `opacity` |

- **Arnold** also writes `root_normal` (`uniform RGB`) with FeatherColor's iridescence. A
  strand attribute whose name is a built-in `curves` parameter (`id`, `mode`, `radius`, `uvs`,
  `opaque`...) is skipped.
- **RenderMan** also writes derived primvars for the hair network shipped with the procedural:
  `rman_melanin`, `rman_redness`, `rman_dye`, `rman_color`, `rman_rgbShare`,
  `rman_specularGainR`, `rman_coneAngleR`, `rman_coneAngleTT`, `rman_coneAngleTRT` and
  `rman_specularOffset` ([RenderMan](/docs/other-renderers#renderman)). Strand attributes that
  share a name with a primvar the procedural writes (`P`, `width`, `id`, `st`, `opacity`...) are
  skipped.
- **USD** makes names valid identifiers: characters other than letters, digits and `_` become `_`,
  and a leading digit gets a `_` prefix. When a name is taken (by another attribute or by `st`,
  `strandId`, `displayColor`, `displayOpacity`, `widths`, `points` or `normals`), a `_2`, `_3`...
  suffix is added. HairColor's per-point RGB, which shares its names with the per-strand RGB, is
  therefore `primvars:colorR_2`, `colorG_2`, `colorB_2`. The whole USD file is specified in
  [USD layout](/docs/usd-layout).
- **Alembic and USD:** the first exported frame determines which attributes the file has; a later
  frame without one of them writes zeros.
- The `.groom` file stores no strands, and so no attributes: the procedurals and `groomlab-cli`
  evaluate the graph and produce them.

### Reading attributes in a shader

| Renderer | Per-strand float | Color | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arnold | `aiUserDataFloat`, **Attribute** = name | `aiUserDataColor`, **Attribute** `hair_color` | `strand_id` is `UINT` user data. |
| RenderMan | `PxrPrimvar`, **Variable Name** = name, **Type** float | `PxrPrimvar` of type color, `hair_color` | `groom.rib` beside the procedural shows the full network. |
| Alembic in another renderer | The renderer's user-data node, attribute = name | `Cd` | Per-curve arbGeomParams; check that the renderer passes uniform-scope attributes to hair shaders. |
| USD | The renderer's primvar reader, primvar = name | `displayColor` | Per-point attributes as `vertex` primvars. |
| Blender | **Attribute** node, type Geometry | `groomlab_color`, `groomlab_point_color` | See [Blender](/docs/blender#rendering). |

**Custom attributes.** To send your own per-strand value to a shader, add an **Attribute** node
before the Output: set `name` (for example `tint`) and drive `value` with a painted map, a texture
or an expression such as `rand(0.2, 0.8)`. It arrives as `tint` in every output above
(`uniform FLOAT` user data in Arnold, a `uniform float` primvar in RenderMan, a uniform-scope
arbGeomParam in Alembic, `primvars:tint` in USD). A **Group** node's mask reaches the renderers
the same way, under the group's name. The Attribute node writes per-strand values only; per-point
custom attributes are not available.

## Instances

The Instance node's meshes (snow, beads, leaves) render through an Arnold `instancer` next to the
curves. With deformation blur on, they move with the hair.

- **`scaleByWidth`** (on by default) scales each instance by the strand width at its position.
- **`pinToRest`** keeps each instance at its rest distance from the root, so strands that stretch
  in a simulation don't slide it toward the tip.
- **`sizeMode`** `fitStrand` sizes each instance to its strand: `fitScale` times the strand's rest
  length along the prototype's +Y, `aspect` times that across. Model the prototype 1 unit long
  along +Y, with its base at the origin.
- Instances bent along their strands (`deform` `alongStrand`) render as one combined mesh instead.

## Scales

Scales (the [Scales node](/docs/fur-and-feathers#scales)) render as one mesh conformed to the skin;
the strands that place them are hidden. The scale mesh carries user data for any surface shader:
`scaleColor` (`varying RGB`: the strand's color, base to tip), `scaleRootColor` (`uniform RGB`),
`scaleId` (`uniform FLOAT`, random 0-1), `scaleUV` (`uniform VECTOR2`, the scalp UV at the root)
and `scaleVariant` (`uniform INT`).

For your own look, connect an **aiUserDataColor** reading `scaleColor` to an
**aiStandardSurface**'s base color, and name its shading group in the node's `material`. Without
one, the built-in plates get a surface shader that reads `scaleColor`. With deformation blur on,
the scales deform with the body.

To use scales elsewhere:

- **Alembic and USD:** the scales are written next to the strands as one animated mesh, with the
  same attributes per vertex (`scaleColor`) and per face (the others).
- **Unreal, exact:** **GroomLab > Export > Scales Mesh (FBX)...** writes a static mesh; skin
  `groomlab.scale_mesh(groom)` to the body in Maya for a skeletal mesh.
- **Unreal, instanced:** **GroomLab > Export > Scales Instancer (USD)...** writes a USD
  PointInstancer of the plates, which Unreal's USD Importer brings in as instanced static meshes.
  Instancers do not bend the plates over the body.
- **Baked maps:** **GroomLab > Utilities > Bake Scale Maps** bakes the scales into the body's UVs as a
  normal map (OpenGL +Y; flip green in Unreal), a 16-bit height map and a color map, one tile per
  UDIM: for distant or tiny scales without geometry.

## Level of detail

For crowds and wide shots, the groom's **Level of Detail** attributes (Attribute Editor >
Rendering) thin and cull strands at render time relative to the render camera:

| Attribute | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `lodDistance` | Reduces the strand count with distance, from `lodNear` to `lodFar` (down to `lodMinDensity`), widening the remaining strands to keep coverage (`lodWidthCompensation`). |
| `cameraCull` | Drops strands entirely outside the camera frustum (plus `cullMargin`). |
| `backfaceCull` | Drops strands whose root faces away from the camera by more than `backfaceAngle` degrees past perpendicular. Off by default; leave margin for long hair hanging into view, silhouettes and shadows. |

The same strands are kept from frame to frame and across motion keys.

## Render overrides

**Rendering > Render Overrides** on the groom change the render only; the viewport is unchanged.
Arnold uses them in both render modes, and exports use them when asked (`render_overrides=True`).

| Attribute | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `renderDensity` | Fraction of the strands rendered, 0-1, thinned the same way as the viewport preview, so the look is kept. |
| `renderWidthScale` | Multiplies every strand's width. |
| `renderLengthScale` | Scales each strand about its root, shape included: 0.5 renders it at half size. |

## Farm renders from a groom file

**GroomLab > Render > Render from Groom File...** exports a `.groom` file with its scalp cache and
switches the groom's `renderMode` to `groomFile` (Maya's progress bar counts the frames; Esc
cancels and removes the half-written file). Exported `.ass` files then contain a
`groomlab_procedural` node that evaluates the groom at render time without Maya, producing the
same curves and user data as the translator.

```python
groomlab.render_from_groom_file(groom, "C:/shots/010/hair.groom", start=1, end=48)
```

The render nodes need `GroomLab/procedurals` on `ARNOLD_PLUGIN_PATH`, which the Maya module sets
up. `groomlab.render_curves(groom)` switches back to exporting curves. There is no license
limit on render nodes.

## Caches for other packages

- **Alembic** and **USD**: **GroomLab > Export > Alembic...** or **USD...** write linear curves
  with widths, strand IDs, root UVs and the strand attributes ([above](#strand-attributes)).
  Maya's progress bar counts the frames; Esc cancels and removes the half-written file, and a
  message says what was written. To render them in RenderMan, Redshift or V-Ray, see
  [Other renderers](/docs/other-renderers).
- **Headless:** `groomlab-cli eval hair.groom -o hair.usda -f 1-48` evaluates a groom file without
  Maya (`--scales instancer` writes the scales as a PointInstancer; `groomlab-cli bake-scales
  hair.groom -o maps/hair_` bakes their skin maps).

## Turntables

**GroomLab > Review > Render Turntable...** renders a turntable of the groom in Arnold in the
background, into the scene's `groomlab/<scene name>/renders` folder (and an `.mp4` if ffmpeg is
available).
