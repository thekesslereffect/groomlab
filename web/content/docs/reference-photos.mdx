<Example name="reference.png" alt="A reference photo lined up with the head mesh" caption="Groom Tools > Photos" optional />

The **Photos** tab in the Groom Tools creates a camera for each reference photo, aligned to the
head mesh, so you can groom while looking through it.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Import a photo

**Import Photo...** creates a camera aimed at the head with the photo on its image plane. The
photo is copied next to the scene, so it moves with the scene.

</Step>
<Step>

### Look through it

**Look Through** shows the photo's camera in the active viewport. Click it again to return to the
previous camera.

</Step>
<Step>

### Line it up

**Align Photo...** opens the **Photos** window on the photo: the photo on the left, the head mesh on
the right, like the Transfer Groom window. You never have to look through the camera to do it.

1. Click a feature on the photo (the corner of an eye, the tip of the nose, an ear), then the same
   feature on the mesh, or the other way round. The Nth point on the photo pairs with the Nth on the
   mesh, numbered on both sides. Drag a point to adjust it; Ctrl+click deletes it and its partner.
2. From four pairs the camera lines up as you place them (the focal length kept). Six or more,
   spread in depth, solve the focal length too, unless **Keep focal length** is on.
3. If you know the lens, set **Focal Length** (mm): Keep focal length turns on and the camera
   lines up again keeping it.

The mesh's outline draws over the photo through the camera, and each pair shows its error in pixels
(a line to where its mesh point lands). A pair that disagrees with the rest turns red: fix that one
first. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo in the window. Below **Align Photo...**, the tab says how
far along the photo is.

You can also line it up by eye: look through it and move the camera.

</Step>
<Step>

### Morph the scalp to the photos (optional)

When the head mesh is not the person in the photos, **Morph to Photos** (in the Photos window,
under **Morph**) shapes the scalp so every lined-up photo's pairs land on their photo points. A
pair seen in one photo moves across that photo's view; a pair seen in two or more photos gets its
depth from them too. The rest of the mesh follows smoothly.

- **Symmetry** (on by default) morphs both sides alike, each pair mirrored across the scalp's
  middle.
- **Stiffness** keeps more of the scalp's own shape: 0 lands every pair exactly, higher moves the
  scalp more as one piece and the pairs fall short.
- Each press starts from the scalp as it was before the first morph, so pressing it again after
  moving a pair never stacks morphs. **Reset Morph** puts the scalp back (undoable).

In Maya the morph is a blendShape on the scalp; in Blender (the Photos tab) a shape key over the
Basis. In the app the groom moves onto the morphed scalp by its UVs.

</Step>
<Step>

### Lock and groom

**Lock** the camera once it is aligned (in the tab or the Photos window): the landmarks no longer
move it, nor does tumbling through it. Set the photo's **Opacity**, place it **In front** of the
mesh or **Behind** it, and brush the guides to match the photo while looking through the camera.

**Zoom in without moving the camera.** Through a locked photo the viewport's navigation zooms and
pans the picture in 2D, the photo and the groom together: the wheel and Alt+right drag zoom, Alt+middle
drag pans, **F** shows the whole view again. The camera stays where you lined it up, and the brushes
work in the zoomed view. Unlocked, the same gestures move the camera, to line it up by eye. In Blender,
a camera view always does this: the wheel zooms, Shift+middle drag pans, **Home** fits the view.

</Step>
</Steps>

```python
import groomlab.reference as ref

cam = ref.import_reference("C:/photos/front.jpg", groom=groom)
ref.set_pair(cam, 0, image=(412, 230), mesh=(0.0, 9.5, 3.1))   # ... 6 pairs (4 keeping the focal length)
ref.solve(cam)          # the camera from the pairs (the Photos window does it as you click)
ref.morph_to_photos(groom, stiffness=0.0, symmetric=True)   # optional; ref.reset_photo_morph(groom)
ref.look_through(cam)
ref.lock_reference(cam)
ref.set_reference_opacity(cam, 0.4)
ref.zoom_view_2d(cam, 3.0, at=(120, 40))   # the view zoomed 3x about a point (pixels from its middle)
ref.reset_view_2d(cam)
```

## Guides from photos (experimental)

<Callout type="info" title="Experimental">
Guides from Photos grows guides that follow the hair in your reference photos. It is a first
version: try it, then brush the result.
</Callout>

**GroomLab > Guides from Photos (experimental)...** opens the Photos window at its **Guides** stage,
or at **Align** while no photo is lined up yet (Groom Tools > Photos has an **Open Guides from
Photos...** button too; the **Align** and **Guides (experimental)** chips at the top left switch
stages). Each stage shows only what its step needs:

- **Align** lines one photo up at a time: the landmarks, the mesh's outline over the photo (**Mesh
  over photo** under it), and in the panel the landmark pairs, the camera and the scalp's shape
  (Morph to Photos). **Next: Guides** moves on.
- **Guides** works with every lined-up photo: the tools **Guide Lines**, **Hair** and **Backdrop**
  above the photo, what it shows below it (**Mask**, **Flow field** and the generated **Guides**,
  root green to tip red), the groom in the mesh view (Maya), and in the panel this photo's mask and lines,
  then Generate's settings and the measured look. A photo that is not lined up yet says so in its
  tab, and Guides from Photos leaves it out.

Each photo keeps its camera with the photo on an image plane, lined up, so renders and Arnold can use
it.

<Example name="reference-window.png" alt="The Photos window at its Guides stage: a photo with the hair mask, guide lines and the generated guides on the left, the scalp with the guides on the right" caption="The Photos window, Guides stage" optional />

<Steps>
<Step>

### Add the photos

**Import Photo...** adds a photo as a tab along the top (**Remove** takes it away; Maya's undo brings
it back). One to three views work: a front, a side and a back, or a single three-quarter view with
symmetry. Each photo has its own landmarks, guide lines, mask and camera. In the photo view the wheel
zooms at the cursor, the middle button (or Alt+drag) pans, and **F** or **Fit** fits the photo.

</Step>
<Step>

### Line each photo up with landmarks

At the **Align** stage, line each photo up as in [Line it up](#line-it-up) above. The mesh can draw
over the photo as its outline or its wireframe (**Mesh over photo** under the photo, with its
**Opacity**).

</Step>
<Step>

### Draw guide lines (optional)

At the **Guides** stage, with **Guide Lines** on, drag along the hair on the photo from root to tip,
as one strand runs: the
line draws green at its root and red at its tip. Guides near it follow its course and its way, and
take its length: a guide growing under a line is as long as the line from there to its tip. Short
lines over a beard give short guides; one long line down the back gives long ones. Ctrl+drag erases.
**Clear Lines** forgets them.

</Step>
<Step>

### Touch up the mask (optional)

The guides grow where the photo's hair mask says there is hair: automatic on a plain backdrop, or an
image you pick with **Mask** (white where the hair is). The automatic mask finds the hair by its
colors, learned from the hair around the top of the head, so a face, a neck and clothes are left out
even when the scalp is the whole head; a beard the hair's color counts as hair (paint it out with
**Backdrop** to leave it bare). **Show > Mask** tints it over the photo and **Flow field** draws the
strand directions read from it. Paint with **Hair** where it missed hair and with **Backdrop** where it
took backdrop for hair (Ctrl swaps them; **Brush**, beside them, sets the size).

Every landmark, guide line and touch-up is one undo step: Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y in the window (or **Undo**
and **Redo**) walk the current photo's edits.

</Step>
<Step>

### Generate

Set **Symmetry** (Auto uses the scalp's own symmetry), **Guide count** and **Max length** (Auto: as
far as the photos show the hair reaching), uncheck **Use this photo** on a photo to leave it out, then
**Generate**. The guides go into the **fromReference** group and are selected, and they draw over
each photo (**Show > Guides**, root green to tip red; in Maya on the scalp in the mesh view too): where they
stray from the hair, draw guide lines there and Generate again. Generate again replaces them; one
Maya undo step each time. While it works, the window shows a wait cursor, greys
out its buttons and says what it is doing (Measured Look's Apply too).

The guides fill the hair's volume: each is lifted off the scalp toward the thickness the photos'
outlines show over it, so voluminous hair is not combed flat. They are as long as the strands the
photos show: each guide runs as far as the strand paths through its root go in the photos (long
hair swept back over the top or falling down the back reaches little past the head's outline, so
the outline alone would make it short), and where both ways along the flow are hair, the one the
photos show hair along much further wins (hair swept back from the forehead, not a fringe over it).

Wavy hair is traced lock by lock. At a wave's crest the strands run across the lock for a moment
(the back of a head of loose curls shows sideways bands), so the guides do not follow each strand's
direction: in each photo the locks' paths are traced from their roots along the hair's coarse flow
(the strand directions averaged over half a wave), each holding its way through the crests and
falling where the photo says little, to where the hair ends. The clearest are kept as locks; the
same lock traced in two photos from the same root is triangulated into a 3D centreline, one seen
in one photo lies on the hair's surface over the scalp. Each centreline is rooted on the scalp (see
below) and scored against the other photos: projected through each photo's camera, how much of it
runs inside the photo's hair along the photo's own strand flow (points the head hides from that
camera don't count). It takes a root's place where the photos support it about as much as the guide
grown there (with symmetry, a lock on the mirrored side competes as its mirror image); the other
guides grow along the locks' flow. So the back falls down in waves and the hair at the hairline sweeps up
and back as the photos show, not into a fringe. Each guide then carries the photos' fine wave along
it (its size, length and phase where a photo shows it; the rest take their neighbours'), and with
Apply each guide's strands clump onto it, with no Curl node over all of them. Long hair that hangs
past the part of the photo first worked on (past the neck, down the back) is worked on wider, and
hair hanging over the neck or the back is hair (a beard, on the face's side, is not).

Every guide starts on the scalp. Whatever made it (grown from a root, a lock's centreline, a stroke,
the comb), the end nearer the scalp is its root (a line that ran tip first is turned round), a root
a little off the scalp is moved onto it and one further off is joined to it by a short segment out
along the scalp's normal (a lock whose both ends are far from the scalp, hanging beside the face, is
left out: more than 0.4 head radii), and a guide that passes through the head is cut where it goes
in (a shallow dip, up to a quarter of the head's radius, that comes out again is pushed back out
instead). The thresholds scale with the head's size.

On curly hair each guide follows a lock. The photos' locks are found first (the strands curling
clearly along each lock) and guides start where they grow from, the rest spread evenly. Where a
photo shows a lock along a guide, the guide moves onto it and takes that lock's curl: its size, the
length of a turn, where along the guide the curl turns, and which way it winds. Guides no photo
shows a lock for take their neighbours' curl. These are rough measures too: check them against the
photos.

**Measured Look** shows what the photos measure against what your graph has: the hair color (and
lighter or darker tips), clumps (how far apart and how tight), curls (or waves: a Curl node with
their size), flyaways, frizz, and where the hair thins. The color is read as a melanin: dark brown
in a softly lit studio photo comes out dark brown. Uncheck what you don't want and click **Apply**: the values are set in one undo step, and
a Clump, Curl, frizz Noise or Flyaway node the graph lacks is added where it usually goes. Thinning
becomes a vertex map (refDensity) on Scatter's density.

These are rough measures from the picture, a starting point to tune by eye. Some things can't be read
from a photo yet: each strand's own color variation (it hides in the shading), frizz in curly hair,
clumps in short fur, and flyaways against a backdrop of the hair's own shade.

</Step>
</Steps>

The standalone app has the same window: **Align Photo...** in its Photos tab, and **Tools > Guides
from Photos (Experimental)...** (photos, landmarks, the camera, guide lines, mask touch-ups and
Generate, saved with the groom; the measured look is Maya's for now).

What works so far: long hair and longer fur, one to three views, symmetry that covers the side a
photo doesn't show, and guide lines that settle which way hair runs and how long it is.

What doesn't yet: ringlets too tight for the photo's size, very short fur (too fine to read in the
photo), and photos lit by colored light (the fitted color takes the light's tint).

How the strand directions are read can be chosen (scripts and agents: `orientation_method` /
`orientation`): **multiscale** (the default) filters each photo at three scales; **variance** uses
one fine bank of 180 filters and rates each pixel by how much one direction stands out (where strands
cross or curl the filters disagree and the pixel counts little). On the wavy test head the default
reads the waves better (the variance reading smooths them out, so the hair is not traced lock by
lock); the confidence picture (`photo_view` with `analysis`) shows where either is unsure. A
bilateral coarse flow (`bilateral_coarse`) keeps neighbouring locks' flows apart at a part.

```python
import groomlab
from groomlab import from_reference as fr
from groomlab.ui import reference_window

w = reference_window.show(groom, stage="guides")  # the Photos window; scripts drive it too:
cam = w.import_photo("C:/photos/front.jpg")
w.add_landmark("photo", (412, 230)); w.add_landmark("mesh", (0.0, 9.5, 3.1))   # ... 4 pairs or more
w.add_flow_stroke([(820, 300), (790, 620)])        # photo pixels, root first

fr.set_reference_hints(groom, cam, [[(820, 300), (790, 620)]])   # or straight onto the groom
fr.set_mask_strokes(groom, cam, [{"points": [(100, 80), (180, 90)], "radius": 12, "include": False}])
result = fr.generate(groom, guide_count=400, symmetry="auto")
fr.measured_look_preview(groom)                  # [{"type", "node", "param", "current", "value"}, ...]
fr.apply_measured_look(groom)                    # or types=["HairColor", "Clump"]
```

Scripts can also pass a painted overlay: a PNG at the photo's size with the strokes opaque on a
transparent layer, dark at the root end and light at the tip (`{"camera": cam, "hint_image":
"flow.png"}` in `groomlab.guides_from_reference`).

## Lock guides from strokes (AI agents)

An [AI agent](/docs/ai-agents) can block a haircut in from the lined-up photos the way an artist
would: a few key locks drawn on the photos, the rest combed and filled in from them.

- **Strokes to guides** (`guides_from_strokes`): draw a stroke along each lock on a photo, root end
  first (on an image of the photo, mark the root end with a dot). Each stroke is lifted through the
  photo's camera onto the hair's outer surface the photos show, rooted on the scalp under its
  root end (a stroke drawn tip first is turned round: the end nearer the scalp is the root), and
  cut where it passes through the head or the photos show the hair end. A stroke on a side photo can be mirrored
  to the other side. With `fill`, the rest of the hair region gets guides grown through the flow
  the strokes make (below), combed where no stroke is near; `fill_mode blend` copies each root's
  nearest strokes' shapes instead.
- **Fitting the strokes in 3D** (`lift fit`, the default): that lift is only the start. Each guide is
  then fitted so that, seen through each photo with a stroke for it, it lies on that stroke, while
  its depth (which one photo cannot see) stays smooth like a stiff rod, out of the head, inside the
  shell and toward the hair's outside as each photo sees it. Rays that only graze the shell no
  longer make depth zig-zags or loops, and a start drawn out on the hair (a side lock seen where it
  comes out from under other hair) is carried back along the lock onto the scalp instead of joined
  to it by a straight spike. The same lock drawn on two photos (front and side, say) becomes one
  guide fitted to both strokes, its depth then known from the photos: strokes are matched when one
  guide fits both and stays where each photo could see it, or label them yourself by giving the
  strokes the same `lock`. A stroke drawn over only part of a lock does not pull the rest of it.
  The answer says, per guide, how far it lies off each of its strokes in pixels and which strokes
  were fused into it; `lift shell` keeps the plain lift.
- **Refitting guides** (`fit_guides`): strokes drawn later on any photo refit the guides lying along
  them in place, keeping their ids (a stroke can name its guide with `guide`).
- **Filling between guides** (`fill_guides`, and the fill of the two tools above): a smooth field of
  hair directions is solved through the hair's shell that follows each guide root to tip, fades out
  a little past each guide's tip and leans out of the scalp near it; new guides grow through it root
  first from roots spread over the scalp, riding about as high as the guides near them, and end where
  the field fades, where the photos show the hair end, or about their neighbours' length. The flow
  between a few drawn locks stays coherent, the back ends where the photos show it end instead of at
  one length, and nothing hangs below the hair. A part (`part`, or `comb.part`) is a seam the flow is
  not blended across; leave it out when the hair has no clear part, since a seam leaves the scalp
  bare along it. The new guides get the comb's wave on top (`wave`), then the lock look.
- **The hair's shell** (`hair_shell`): the hair's outer surface round the head from every lined-up
  photo's hair mask, and how thick the hair is over each part of the head.
- **Combing** (`comb_guides`): a haircut blocked in without strokes: a part, the front swept back
  (or out, or forward), the sides back over the ears, the back falling, each guide riding the hair's
  shell with a wave across the flow. By default every third guide is combed so and all of them grow
  through the field those make (as above), which keeps the flow from blending across the part and
  ends the hair at the shell instead of hanging by the ears and nape; `mode comb` combs each one alone.
- The lock look (`apply_look`): the guides as the clumps' centers, finer clumps inside them, the tips
  trimmed unevenly and no hair below the lowest guide roots.

The strokes and the comb work on the head as it is (after Morph to Photos) and scale with its size.
They are a starting point: look at the guides through each photo's camera and fix single guides with
`reshape_guides`. `score_guides` rates any guides against the photos (how much of each runs inside
the photos' hair along their strand flow, photo by photo) and lists the worst ones to fix first.
